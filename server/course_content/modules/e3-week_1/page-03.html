Engineering Standards & Handbooks Resources

A curated, annotated list of engineering standards resources and handbooks relevant to aerospace experimental methods — organized by measurement domain (fluid mechanics, structural, thermal, vibration). ASME V&V 10-2006 is listed first as the primary course standard. Additional pointers include AIAA standards, ASME PTC documents, ASTM measurement standards, relevant MIL-HDBK documents, and recommended handbooks (including Coleman & Steele 4th edition for uncertainty). Serves as the reference resource for both the W11 preliminary proposal and the W12 instrumentation justification report. Should be reviewed and updated annually as standards are revised.

9 min Readings

Learning Objectives

  • Apply formal experimental design vocabulary — independent and dependent variables, replication, randomization, blocking, control, and confounding factors — to an aerospace measurement problem — CLO 1
  • State a formal hypothesis that defines what a proposed experiment is expected to show, what data would confirm it, and what data would challenge it — CLO 1, CLO 3
  • Identify and cite ASME V&V 10-2006 and at least one additional relevant engineering standard, handbook, or prior publication applicable to their E3 design challenge — CLO 1, CLO 6
  • Describe what is already established in prior literature about their measurement domain and articulate what gap or question their experiment addresses — CLO 6

Engineering References: Optional Resources for Experimental Design

Use a source when it informs a real design decision

This reading is a reference for the Tier 3 proposal. A formal engineering standards citation is not required. Choose a credible outside source that helps your team make or evaluate a design choice. Depending on the experiment, a useful source may be a manufacturer datasheet, application note, handbook, technical paper, measurement method, platform manual, or relevant standard.

Use sources with a question in mind

Before searching, identify what you need to learn. Examples include sensor range or bandwidth, an installation effect, an established measurement method, a model assumption, or a practical DAQ limit. Prefer a source that directly supports that question.

When you use a source, briefly state:

  • what information it provides;
  • how that information affects your proposed design; and
  • what it does not establish.

A datasheet can support a sensor’s stated range and accuracy. It does not, by itself, establish that the sensor is suitable for your setup or that the entire measurement chain is accurate. A technical paper may show a method in one context without proving it applies to yours.

Standards and handbooks

Use a standard or handbook when its scope fits your experiment. Measurement-uncertainty guidance, test codes, and domain-specific standards can be valuable, but no particular standard is required for every proposal. A source should inform the work rather than appear as a citation added only to satisfy a count.

Takeaway: A relevant, credible source can strengthen a design decision. Your proposal needs one meaningful outside resource for the CLO6 investigation; it does not need a formal standards analysis or literature review.
