Peer Evaluation and Engineering Critique Worksheet

A reading-format worksheet used during W14 oral defenses and sample-work critique. Students apply the same engineering standard to peer work and AI-generated or instructor-selected sample work: hypothesis testability, instrumentation adequacy, DAQ/sampling/filtering defensibility, uncertainty support for the validation claim, validation metric and intended-use fit, feasibility and safety, experimentally informed model update pathway, one specific strength, one specific weakness, and one actionable improvement. The worksheet makes critique quality auditable and keeps feedback tied to technical evidence rather than surface polish. ## 3. Bridge to Next Week The oral defenses and peer evaluations are complete. Week 15 is not another deliverable week — it is a reflection and integration week where students step back from the work they have produced and map it explicitly to the course framework, articulate what they learned about themselves as experimentalists, and close the arc that opened with a single question in Week 1.

Learning Objectives

  • Deliver a structured oral defense of an experimental design and V&V plan to a technical audience, responding to questions about instrumentation choices, uncertainty analysis, and validation logic — CLO 5
  • Evaluate a peer's experimental design using a structured rubric, identifying specific strengths, genuine weaknesses, and concrete improvement recommendations — CLO 5, CLO 6
  • Apply technical communication principles — structured arguments, figures as arguments, results separated from interpretation, limitations stated explicitly — in the oral and written communication of experimental design work — CLO 5
  • Analyze sample experimental design work — from prior course iterations, professional reports, or AI-generated content — using the same evaluation rubric applied to peer presentations, demonstrating that critique quality is independent of the source of the work being evaluated — CLO 5, CLO 6

AMVF Mapping and Reflection Worksheet

The course ends by asking what changed in your engineering judgment

The final reflection is not a victory lap; it is evidence that you now think differently about experiments.

Week 15 closes ASEN 3501 by returning to the question that started the course: how do you know this prediction is right? Your answer should be more disciplined now. It should include a model claim, a measurement strategy, uncertainty, a validation metric, intended use, and a willingness to revise the model when evidence demands it. Use this worksheet to map that growth and to prepare your CLO 6 and course reflections.
