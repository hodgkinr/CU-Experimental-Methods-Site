Calibration vs. Validation — A Distinction That Matters

Introduces the calibration-vs-validation distinction at the conceptual level: calibration adjusts a sensor's output to match a known reference (it is a measurement activity), while validation tests whether a model makes accurate predictions (it is a modeling activity). Conflating the two is a common and consequential error. This distinction is introduced in Week 3, deepened in E2 Week 8, and operationalized in the E3 V&V plan. Students encounter a case study where a "calibrated" result was incorrectly treated as a "validated" model. This lecture covers the conceptual distinction and the error pattern only (slides 2–5 in the lecture outline). Anomalous data prediction content continues in Lecture 3. See `lectures/E1_week_3/E1_W3_L2_presentation_outline.txt`. *(Note: shortened by V&V Executor 2026-05-19 per instructor approval — anomalous data content moved to W3 L3.)*

Learning Objectives

  • Apply a named validation metric to compare a measured outcome to a model prediction within defined uncertainty bounds — CLO 2, CLO 3
  • Explain the distinction between calibration and validation and articulate why conflating them undermines experimental credibility — CLO 3
  • Evaluate agreement between two measurements of the same quantity using different sensors or methods — CLO 1, CLO 2
  • Identify at least one model-form limitation from residual inspection and articulate its implication — CLO 3
