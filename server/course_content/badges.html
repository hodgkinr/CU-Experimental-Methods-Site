Aerospace Experimental Methods

Badges

Each badge is tied to one Course Learning Outcome. The assessments below are the major summative places where evidence for that badge can be produced and defended.

CLO 1 Mastery Badge

Design and conduct experiments

Design and conduct aerospace experiments by applying measurement fundamentals, instrumentation principles, and best practices in small team environments.

Major summative assessment opportunities

  • Lab 1: Power, Voltage, and the Language of UncertaintyWeeks 2-5

    Students conduct a controlled bench experiment using a power supply, resistor, and multimeter while documenting proper measurement practice. The simple circuit keeps the focus on careful execution and defensible measurement records.

  • Tier 1: Forward Analysis, Reverse Prediction, and Combined SynthesisWeeks 5-8

    Students execute assigned experiments, identify variables and confounders, record measurements with uncertainty, and compare sensor methods. The assignment moves from following a procedure toward disciplined experimental conduct.

  • Tier 2: Predict, Execute, Analyze, ImproveWeeks 8-11

    Groups prepare, run, and document a more complex aerospace experiment under practical lab constraints. They must record calibration status, anomalies, setup decisions, and evidence while the experiment is happening.

  • Final Lab: Paper Experimental Design and V&V PlanWeeks 12-15

    Students design a feasible experiment with variables, controls, confounding factors, safety considerations, and a bounded test matrix. The assignment evaluates whether their planned procedure could be executed by an engineering team.

CLO 2 Mastery Badge

Apply uncertainty and statistics

Apply statistical methods and uncertainty analysis to quantify experimental error and establish confidence in results.

Major summative assessment opportunities

  • Lab 1: Power, Voltage, and the Language of UncertaintyWeeks 2-5

    A central objective of this assignment is to quantify uncertainty in power estimates using datasheet-based Type B inputs, first-order propagation, and Monte Carlo simulation. Students must state estimates with units, interval type, method, and coverage basis.

  • Tier 1: Forward Analysis, Reverse Prediction, and Combined SynthesisWeeks 5-8

    Students propagate uncertainty for derived quantities, rank dominant error sources, and use sensor specifications before seeing data. The work reinforces uncertainty as part of the argument rather than a calculation appended at the end.

  • Tier 2: Predict, Execute, Analyze, ImproveWeeks 8-11

    Students estimate uncertainty before hardware access and then revisit the uncertainty budget after collecting data. They must decide whether the measurement chain can resolve the intended quantity and identify dominant uncertainty sources.

  • Final Lab: Paper Experimental Design and V&V PlanWeeks 12-15

    Students perform prospective uncertainty analysis before any data exists and judge whether the expected measurement performance is adequate. The uncertainty work informs design decisions rather than merely describing past measurements.

CLO 3 Mastery Badge

Integrate models and data

Integrate theoretical models with experimental data to validate and evaluate aerospace system behavior.

Major summative assessment opportunities

  • Tier 1: Forward Analysis, Reverse Prediction, and Combined SynthesisWeeks 5-8

    The assignment asks students to compare measured data to predictive models and interpret discrepancies using named validation metrics. The blind prediction phase makes the model-data relationship explicit before results are available.

  • Tier 2: Predict, Execute, Analyze, ImproveWeeks 8-11

    The committed prediction, acceptance criterion, residual analysis, and improvement proposal all connect model behavior to measured evidence. Students learn to distinguish ordinary scatter from model-form discrepancy or setup effects.

  • Final Lab: Paper Experimental Design and V&V PlanWeeks 12-15

    The final plan must define a predictive model claim, validation metrics, acceptance criteria, and expected response features. Students explain how future data would inform, challenge, or improve the model.

CLO 4 Mastery Badge

Implement signal and DAQ methods

Implement signal conditioning, sampling, and data acquisition techniques to ensure accurate experimental measurements.

Major summative assessment opportunities

  • Tier 2: Predict, Execute, Analyze, ImproveWeeks 8-11

    Tier 2 makes sensor range, DAQ configuration, calibration status, and measurement-chain adequacy part of the central assignment. Students must show that instrumentation choices can support the intended validation claim.

  • Final Lab: Paper Experimental Design and V&V PlanWeeks 12-15

    Students specify the full measurement chain, including sensors, signal conditioning, sampling rate, DAQ range and resolution, filtering, and installation effects. These choices must be justified using expected signal levels and practical constraints.

CLO 5 Mastery Badge

Communicate results

Communicate experimental results effectively through technical reports and presentations, with clear description of methods, results, and limitations.

Major summative assessment opportunities

  • Lab 1: Power, Voltage, and the Language of UncertaintyWeeks 2-5

    The brief report and oral interview require students to explain what they measured, what uncertainty means, and what limitations remain. This directly practices concise technical communication about experimental claims.

  • Tier 1: Forward Analysis, Reverse Prediction, and Combined SynthesisWeeks 5-8

    Students communicate their analysis through an individual report, a prediction memo, and a combined technical presentation. The paired synthesis requires them to explain what predictions got right, what they missed, and why.

  • Tier 2: Predict, Execute, Analyze, ImproveWeeks 8-11

    The group report, quad chart, Q&A, and peer evaluations require compact, defensible communication of prediction-to-outcome logic. Students practice defending conclusions by tracing evidence rather than restating claims.

  • Final Lab: Paper Experimental Design and V&V PlanWeeks 12-15

    Students produce written design artifacts and defend the design orally to a technical audience. The assignment emphasizes professional communication of methods, limitations, tradeoffs, and evidence.

CLO 6 Mastery Badge

Create beyond provided methods

Identify, evaluate, and create experimental methods or instrumentation beyond those provided by the instructor to foster independent professional growth.

Major summative assessment opportunities

  • Tier 2: Predict, Execute, Analyze, ImproveWeeks 8-11

    Students propose specific improvements to an existing experiment, tied to evidence from model comparison, instrumentation limits, or measurement strategy. This begins the shift from using provided methods to evaluating how methods could be better.

  • Final Lab: Paper Experimental Design and V&V PlanWeeks 12-15

    This is the primary CLO 6 demonstration: students must investigate at least one sensor, method, platform, standard, or analysis approach beyond the provided course catalog. They compare alternatives and justify whether the outside method improves the design.
