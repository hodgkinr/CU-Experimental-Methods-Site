Tier 2 Candidate

Aeroelasticity

Use wind-tunnel sensor uncertainty to select informative airspeed or dynamic-pressure conditions, then determine infinite-wing Cl and Cd versus angle of attack and recommend a defensible test envelope.

Procedure

Procedure - Aeroelasticity Tier 2 Experiment

> Lab: Wind-tunnel condition selection and infinite-wing aerodynamic coefficients > > Hardware: PILOT low-speed wind tunnel, Clark Y-14 infinite-wing model, pressure scanner, and tunnel sensors > > Software: LabVIEW wind-tunnel interface and MATLAB analysis tools

Purpose

The group will evaluate the wind-tunnel measurement system as an engineering consultant. Students first use sensor values and uncertainty information to choose informative tunnel airspeed or dynamic-pressure conditions. They then apply those selected conditions to infinite-wing data over angle of attack and calculate \(C_l\), \(C_d\), and their uncertainties.

There is no separate auxiliary-tunnel activity in the Tier 2 experiment. Finite-wing structural and whiffle-tree analysis is reference material only and is not required student work.

Safety and setup

Follow the instructor’s wind-tunnel safety briefing. Secure the airfoil and all pressure tubing before running the tunnel, keep loose objects outside the test section, and return the tunnel to zero speed before changing the angle of attack. Confirm that the model, pressure scanner, and tunnel control interface are operating before collecting the committed dataset.

Day 1 - Prediction and test matrix

  1. Inspect the pressure, temperature, atmospheric-pressure, angle-of-attack, and airspeed measurement chain.
  2. Record the sensor models, ranges, calibration information, sampling settings, units, and any instructor-provided uncertainty values.
  3. Review the pressure-port mapping, measured-versus-commanded condition fields, and supplied coefficient equations.
  4. Use the sensor specifications to construct an initial uncertainty envelope primarily versus measured tunnel airspeed or dynamic pressure.
  5. Define the decision rule for informative, marginal, and uncertainty-dominated conditions.
  6. Select the angle-of-attack and tunnel-condition matrix to test, documenting why the selected conditions are worth the available time.

Day 2 - Infinite-wing pressure measurements

  1. Confirm the Clark Y-14 model is mounted securely and that the active pressure-port set is connected correctly.
  2. Confirm the pressure scanner and tunnel airspeed channels are zeroed or tared according to the instructor’s procedure.
  3. Set the first assigned angle of attack and close the tunnel test section.
  4. Run the assigned freestream velocities in the approved order. Record the actual measured velocity, commanded velocity, angle of attack, atmospheric conditions, and file name for every condition.
  5. Collect the chord-wise pressure data after the tunnel has reached steady operation. Preserve the raw data file without overwriting it.
  6. Return the tunnel to zero speed before changing angle of attack.
  7. Repeat for the remaining assigned angles of attack and velocities.

The test matrix should contain enough conditions to show how the pressure and derived lift uncertainty change with velocity and angle of attack. The group should not spend limited time collecting redundant points in a region already predicted to be dominated by sensor resolution.

Day 3 - Data reduction and uncertainty analysis

  1. Convert the pressure channels to physical units using the supplied calibration information.
  2. Calculate measured \(U_\infty\), \(\rho_\infty\), \(q_\infty\), and \(C_p\) for each condition.
  3. Use the pressure-port mapping and supplied geometry to calculate \(C_n\), \(C_a\), \(C_l\), and \(C_d\).
  4. Run Taylor-series and Monte Carlo uncertainty sweeps versus measured airspeed or dynamic pressure, using the instructor-approved model assumptions.
  5. Compare uncertainty contributions from differential pressure, atmospheric pressure, temperature, angle of attack, surface pressure, and any other available channels.
  6. Compare the physics-based coefficient prediction with measured coefficients and prepare residual plots.

Day 4 - Targeted validation and recommendation

  1. Inspect the preliminary absolute and relative uncertainty envelopes.
  2. Select targeted repeat conditions near a predicted low-uncertainty region, a high-uncertainty region, and any proposed boundary.
  3. Collect only the additional points needed to test the recommendation or diagnose a discrepancy.
  4. Compare measured infinite-wing results with the committed prediction.
  5. Apply the stated decision rule to classify conditions as informative, marginal, or uncertainty-dominated.
  6. Quantify one improvement, such as pressure-sensor range, resolution, calibration, sampling, or atmospheric-condition measurement, and show how it changes the useful test envelope.

Required data products

  • Raw pressure and tunnel-condition files for every collected condition.
  • A measurement-chain diagram with the physical quantity, sensor output, calibration, and data-reduction role of each channel.
  • Infinite-wing pressure, \(C_p\), \(C_n\), \(C_a\), \(C_l\), and \(C_d\) results across the selected angle-of-attack and tunnel-condition matrix.
  • Absolute scaled systematic standard uncertainty and normalized systematic standard uncertainty curves versus measured airspeed or dynamic pressure.
  • A comparison of the Taylor-series and Monte Carlo results at representative conditions.
  • Residuals and a discussion separating sensor uncertainty from model-form differences.
  • A client recommendation identifying informative, marginal, and uncertainty-dominated regions

using the stated decision rule, plus a quantified improvement.

Data-quality checks

  • Verify the pressure and airspeed channels are zeroed before each run.
  • Confirm that the actual measured velocity, not just the commanded velocity, is used in the data reduction.
  • Check for saturated channels, disconnected pressure ports, implausible pressure distributions, and nonsteady tunnel operation.
  • Preserve the original raw files and record every deletion, filtering choice, or excluded sample.
  • State clearly which conditions were commanded and which were measured.
  • State the decision rule and any instructor-defined limits used to classify conditions.

Model / Derivation

Theoretical Framework - Aeroelasticity Tier 2 Experiment

This framework supports the revised student application: use the tunnel measurement chain and uncertainty model to select conditions, then reduce infinite-wing data to \(C_l\) and \(C_d\), compare prediction and measurement, and recommend a test envelope.

1. Freestream model

\[ \rho_\infty=\frac{p_\infty}{R_{\mathrm{air}}T_\infty}, \qquad V_\infty=\sqrt{\frac{2\Delta p}{\rho_\infty}}, \qquad q_\infty=\frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty^2. \]

Use \(R_{\mathrm{air}}=287\ \mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}\) unless the instructor supplies another value. Keep commanded and measured tunnel conditions separate; use measured quantities in the data reduction.

2. Pressure-to-coefficient model

\[ C_p=\frac{p-p_\infty}{q_\infty}. \]

Integrate the measured chord-wise pressure distribution using the supplied port coordinates:

\[ C_n=-\frac{1}{c}\oint C_p\,dy, \qquad C_a=\frac{1}{c}\oint C_p\,dz. \]

Rotate the sectional force coefficients:

\[ C_l=C_n\cos\alpha-C_a\sin\alpha, \qquad C_d=C_n\sin\alpha+C_a\cos\alpha. \]

The infinite-wing lift per unit span, when needed for interpretation, is \(\ell'_\infty=q_\infty c C_l\). It is not a finite-wing structural calculation.

3. Uncertainty and experimental design

Use measured airspeed \(V_\infty\) or dynamic pressure \(q_\infty\) as the primary sweep variable and angle of attack as a second condition variable. Propagate uncertainty from differential pressure, atmospheric pressure, temperature, angle of attack, surface-pressure channels, port geometry, and any other instructor-designated inputs.

Report

\[ \mathrm{SSU}_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q)=b_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q), \qquad \mathrm{NSU}_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q)=\frac{b_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q)}{|y(q)|}, \]

for selected outputs such as \(V_\infty\), \(q_\infty\), \(C_l\), or \(C_d\). Near a nominal zero, use absolute uncertainty and do not interpret the relative curve through the zero. Use Taylor-series sensitivity coefficients and Monte Carlo propagation at representative conditions.

4. Prediction, residuals, and model form

Compare the physics-based coefficient prediction with measured \(C_l\) and \(C_d\). Plot residuals versus angle of attack and the selected tunnel condition. Interpret persistent differences as possible model-form differences rather than silently absorbing them into sensor uncertainty.

Reference-only material

Finite-wing spanwise loading, spar/beam parameters, structural deflection, whiffle-tree analysis, finite-wing validation, and finite-wing structural uncertainty are outside the revised student objective. Related files may remain in the repository as historical or instructor-reference material.

Model Equations Reference - Aeroelasticity Tier 2 Experiment

This reference supplies the equations needed for the Tier 2 analysis. Students use the equations to select tunnel conditions, reduce the infinite-wing data to aerodynamic coefficients, compare prediction and measurement, and propagate uncertainty.

1. Freestream density, dynamic pressure, and velocity

\[ \rho_\infty=\frac{p_\infty}{R_{\mathrm{air}}T_\infty}, \qquad q_\infty=\frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty^2. \]

For a Pitot-static measurement,

\[ V_\infty=\sqrt{\frac{2\Delta p}{\rho_\infty}} =\sqrt{\frac{2R_{\mathrm{air}}T_\infty\Delta p}{p_\infty}}. \]

Use \(R_{\mathrm{air}}=287\ \mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}\), unless the instructor supplies another value.

2. Pressure coefficient

\[ C_p=\frac{p-p_\infty}{q_\infty}. \]

Here \(p\) is the local surface pressure, \(p_\infty\) is the freestream static pressure, and \(q_\infty\) is dynamic pressure. Confirm the sign convention used by the supplied pressure files before plotting.

3. Sectional force coefficients

Using the pressure-port geometry and a documented trapezoidal integration,

\[ C_n=-\frac{1}{c}\oint C_p\,dy, \qquad C_a=\frac{1}{c}\oint C_p\,dz. \]

The discrete implementation is

\[ C_n=-\sum_i\frac{C_{p,i}+C_{p,i+1}}{2}\frac{\Delta y_i}{c}, \qquad C_a=\sum_i\frac{C_{p,i}+C_{p,i+1}}{2}\frac{\Delta z_i}{c}. \]

The lift and pressure-drag coefficients are

\[ C_l=C_n\cos\alpha-C_a\sin\alpha, \qquad C_d=C_n\sin\alpha+C_a\cos\alpha. \]

Use radians inside trigonometric functions.

4. Infinite-wing aerodynamic coefficients

Students test the infinite wing at multiple assigned values of \(\alpha\) and measured \(V_\infty\) or \(q_\infty\). Use the pressure integration equations to determine how \(C_l\), \(C_d\), lift-related quantities, and their uncertainty change with the test condition.

5. Condition-dependent uncertainty

The primary sweep variable is measured tunnel airspeed \(V_\infty\) or dynamic pressure \(q_\infty\), with angle of attack varied as a second test variable. Propagate uncertainty from differential pressure, atmospheric pressure, temperature, angle of attack, surface pressure channels, pressure-port geometry, and any other instructor-designated inputs.

For each condition, report both

\[ \mathrm{SSU}_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q)=b_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q) \]

and

\[ \mathrm{NSU}_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q) =\frac{b_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q)}{|y(q)|}, \]

for the selected quantity \(y\), such as \(V_\infty\), \(q_\infty\), \(C_l\), or \(C_d\). Use the normalized-sensitivity guide for the Taylor-series calculation and the same data-reduction model for Monte Carlo. Near a nominal zero, use absolute uncertainty and do not interpret the relative curve through the zero.

6. Prediction, residuals, and model-form differences

Compare the physics-based prediction with the measured \(C_l\) and \(C_d\) results at the selected conditions. Plot residuals versus angle of attack and the selected tunnel condition. Do not fold persistent model-form differences into sensor uncertainty without explaining the distinction.

Reference-only material

Finite-wing spanwise loading, spar/beam parameters, structural deflection, whiffle-tree analysis, finite-wing validation, and finite-wing structural uncertainty are outside the revised student objective. Any finite-wing files in the repository are historical or instructor-reference material only.

Notes

Additional Notes - Aeroelasticity Tier 2

Pedagogical purpose

The branch uses uncertainty analysis to select tunnel conditions before applying those conditions to an infinite-wing pressure survey. The main learning move is from pointwise pressure uncertainty to a condition-dependent recommendation about measured airspeed or dynamic pressure and angle of attack.

Recommended emphasis

  • Treat the measured velocity and angle of attack as test conditions, not merely metadata.
  • Use the pressure measurement chain to explain why the uncertainty changes across the test matrix.
  • Use the infinite-wing result as the physics-based comparison.
  • Keep sensor-input uncertainty, model-form discrepancy, and commanded-versus-measured

conditions separate.

  • Require a stated decision rule for appropriate, marginal, and uncertainty-dominated

conditions.

  • Require both scaled output uncertainty and normalized uncertainty contributions.

Four-day planning note

Day 1 should be measurement-chain modeling, uncertainty-based condition selection, and prediction. Day 2 should be the infinite-wing pressure survey at selected conditions. Day 3 should be coefficient reduction, uncertainty sweeps, and residual analysis. Day 4 should be targeted validation and the client recommendation.

Instructor decisions still required

  • Which angles of attack and velocities are assigned?
  • Which infinite-wing pressure channels and sample-data columns are released?
  • What uncertainty threshold defines an acceptable test condition?
  • Which sensor or setup improvements are students allowed to evaluate?

Interpretation prompt

Ask students whether a lower normalized uncertainty is always preferable. The intended answer is no: a condition also needs to be physically valid, safe, relevant to the client objective, and supported by an appropriate model.
