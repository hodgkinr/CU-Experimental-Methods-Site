Tier 2 Candidate

Aeroelasticity

Evaluate wind-tunnel pressure measurements, infinite- and finite-wing lift estimates, and uncertainty in predicted flexible-wing tip deflection across the useful freestream-velocity envelope.

Procedure

Procedure - Aeroelasticity Tier 2 Experiment

> Lab: Infinite-wing pressure measurements and finite-wing tip-deflection prediction > > Hardware: PILOT low-speed wind tunnel, Clark Y-14 infinite-wing model, supplied finite-wing dataset, and supplied structural model > > Software: LabVIEW wind-tunnel interface and MATLAB analysis tools

Purpose

The group will evaluate the wind-tunnel measurement system as an engineering consultant. The student-collected experiment varies angle of attack and freestream velocity for the infinite-wing model. The finite-wing data needed for the tip-deflection prediction are supplied at one assigned angle of attack and airspeed because the available laboratory time does not support a full finite-wing survey.

There is no separate auxiliary-tunnel activity in the Tier 2 experiment. The structural equations and geometry are supplied; students do not design or derive the whiffle tree.

Safety and setup

Follow the instructor’s wind-tunnel safety briefing. Secure the airfoil and all pressure tubing before running the tunnel, keep loose objects outside the test section, and return the tunnel to zero speed before changing the angle of attack. Confirm that the model, pressure scanner, and tunnel control interface are operating before collecting the committed dataset.

Day 1 - Prediction and test matrix

  1. Inspect the pressure, temperature, atmospheric-pressure, angle-of-attack, and airspeed measurement chain.
  2. Record the sensor models, ranges, calibration information, sampling settings, units, and any instructor-provided uncertainty values.
  3. Review the supplied finite-wing dataset and structural equations.
  4. Select the assigned infinite-wing angles of attack and freestream velocities.
  5. Predict the pressure distribution, sectional \(C_l\), lift, and the finite-wing tip deflection at the supplied finite-wing condition.
  6. Prepare the initial uncertainty envelope versus freestream velocity and identify the conditions most worth testing.

Day 2 - Infinite-wing pressure measurements

  1. Confirm the Clark Y-14 model is mounted securely and that the active pressure-port set is connected correctly.
  2. Confirm the pressure scanner and tunnel airspeed channels are zeroed or tared according to the instructor’s procedure.
  3. Set the first assigned angle of attack and close the tunnel test section.
  4. Run the assigned freestream velocities in the approved order. Record the actual measured velocity, commanded velocity, angle of attack, atmospheric conditions, and file name for every condition.
  5. Collect the chord-wise pressure data after the tunnel has reached steady operation. Preserve the raw data file without overwriting it.
  6. Return the tunnel to zero speed before changing angle of attack.
  7. Repeat for the remaining assigned angles of attack and velocities.

The test matrix should contain enough conditions to show how the pressure and derived lift uncertainty change with velocity and angle of attack. The group should not spend limited time collecting redundant points in a region already predicted to be dominated by sensor resolution.

Day 3 - Data reduction and finite-wing prediction

  1. Convert the pressure channels to physical units using the supplied calibration information.
  2. Calculate \(q_\infty\), \(C_p\), \(C_n\), \(C_a\), and \(C_l\) for each infinite-wing condition.
  3. Use the supplied finite-wing dataset at its single assigned angle of attack and airspeed to obtain the spanwise \(C_l(z)\) distribution.
  4. Convert the spanwise finite-wing distribution to lift load intensity \(w(z)\).
  5. Use the supplied beam equation to calculate finite-wing tip deflection at the supplied finite-wing condition.
  6. Compare the infinite-wing baseline model with the finite-wing prediction and identify the physical reason for their difference.
  7. Run the Taylor-series and Monte Carlo uncertainty sweeps versus freestream velocity using the instructor-approved model assumptions.

Day 4 - Targeted validation and recommendation

  1. Inspect the preliminary absolute and relative uncertainty envelopes.
  2. Select targeted repeat conditions near a predicted low-uncertainty region, a high-uncertainty region, and any proposed boundary.
  3. Collect only the additional points needed to test the recommendation or diagnose a discrepancy.
  4. Compare measured infinite-wing results with the committed prediction.
  5. Report the finite-wing prediction and its uncertainty at the supplied finite-wing condition.
  6. Quantify one improvement, such as pressure-sensor range, resolution, calibration, sampling, or atmospheric-condition measurement, and show how it changes the useful test envelope.

Required data products

  • Raw pressure and tunnel-condition files for every collected condition.
  • A measurement-chain diagram with the physical quantity, sensor output, calibration, and data-reduction role of each channel.
  • Infinite-wing pressure, \(C_p\), \(C_l\), and lift results across the assigned angle-of-attack and velocity matrix.
  • The supplied finite-wing spanwise dataset and the resulting tip-deflection prediction at its single assigned condition.
  • Absolute scaled systematic standard uncertainty and normalized systematic standard uncertainty curves versus freestream velocity.
  • A comparison of the Taylor-series and Monte Carlo results at representative conditions.
  • A client recommendation identifying appropriate and inappropriate velocity/angle-of-attack regions and a quantified improvement.

Data-quality checks

  • Verify the pressure and airspeed channels are zeroed before each run.
  • Confirm that the actual measured velocity, not just the commanded velocity, is used in the data reduction.
  • Check for saturated channels, disconnected pressure ports, implausible pressure distributions, and nonsteady tunnel operation.
  • Preserve the original raw files and record every deletion, filtering choice, or excluded sample.
  • State clearly that the finite-wing result is based on one supplied finite-wing condition rather than a student-collected finite-wing sweep.

Model / Derivation

Theoretical Framework - Aeroelasticity Tier 2 Experiment

1. Freestream model

Using the ideal-gas relation and the measured pressure difference,

\[ \rho_\infty=\frac{p_\infty}{R_{\mathrm{air}}T_\infty}, \qquad V_\infty=\sqrt{\frac{2\Delta p}{\rho_\infty}} =\sqrt{\frac{2R_{\mathrm{air}}T_\infty\Delta p}{p_\infty}}. \]

The dynamic pressure is

\[ q_\infty=\frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty^2. \]

Use \(R_{\mathrm{air}}=287\ \mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}\) unless the instructor supplies another value.

2. Pressure-to-lift model

The pressure coefficient is

\[ C_p=\frac{p-p_\infty}{q_\infty}. \]

Integrate the measured chord-wise pressure distribution using the supplied port coordinates:

\[ C_n=-\frac{1}{c}\oint C_p\,dy, \qquad C_a=\frac{1}{c}\oint C_p\,dz. \]

Rotate the sectional force coefficients into the freestream frame:

\[ C_l=C_n\cos\alpha-C_a\sin\alpha, \qquad C_d=C_n\sin\alpha+C_a\cos\alpha. \]

For the infinite-wing baseline, the lift per unit span is

\[ \ell'_\infty=q_\infty c C_l. \]

Students collect these quantities at multiple assigned values of \(\alpha\) and \(V_\infty\).

3. Supplied finite-wing model

The instructor provides a finite-wing spanwise pressure dataset at one assigned angle of attack and airspeed. Use the supplied span stations to determine \(C_l(z)\) and

\[ w(z)=q_\infty c C_l(z). \]

Interpolate between stations using a documented method. The finite-wing dataset is intentionally a single-condition dataset; it is not a student-collected full test envelope.

4. Supplied structural model

The instructor provides the structural geometry and beam properties. For a cantilever of length \(L\), use

\[ \nu_{\mathrm{tip}} = \frac{1}{6EI}\int_0^L w(z)z^2(3L-z)\,dz. \]

For a discrete dataset, evaluate the integral with a documented numerical quadrature rule. If an equivalent point load is supplied, use

\[ \nu_{\mathrm{tip}} = \frac{F_{\mathrm{eq}}x_{\mathrm{eq}}^2(3L-x_{\mathrm{eq}})}{6EI}. \]

Students do not derive or redesign a whiffle tree and do not re-derive the supplied beam equation.

5. Uncertainty and experimental design

The output of interest is \(\nu_{\mathrm{tip}}\). Sweep \(V_\infty\) over the approved range and hold \(\alpha\) at an assigned value or use it as a second approved condition variable. Propagate uncertainty from pressure, atmospheric pressure, temperature, angle of attack, spanwise position, and structural parameters.

Report the scaled systematic standard uncertainty

\[ \mathrm{SSU}_{\nu,\mathrm{sys}}(V_\infty)=b_{\nu,\mathrm{sys}}(V_\infty) \]

and the normalized systematic standard uncertainty

\[ \mathrm{NSU}_{\nu,\mathrm{sys}}(V_\infty)=\frac{b_{\nu,\mathrm{sys}}(V_\infty)}{|\nu_{\mathrm{tip}}(V_\infty)|}. \]

Use both Taylor-series sensitivity coefficients and Monte Carlo propagation at representative conditions. Compare the infinite-wing baseline with the supplied finite-wing result and explain how the finite-wing information changes the prediction and the recommended test envelope.

Tier 2 theoretical framework - working draft for instructor review

Model Equations Reference - Aeroelasticity Tier 2 Experiment

This reference supplies the equations needed for the Tier 2 analysis. Students use the equations to reduce the infinite-wing data, process the supplied finite-wing data, predict tip deflection, and propagate uncertainty. The structural equations are supplied; students do not derive or redesign the whiffle tree.

1. Freestream density, dynamic pressure, and velocity

\[ \rho_\infty=\frac{p_\infty}{R_{\mathrm{air}}T_\infty}, \qquad q_\infty=\frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty^2. \]

For a Pitot-static measurement,

\[ V_\infty=\sqrt{\frac{2\Delta p}{\rho_\infty}} =\sqrt{\frac{2R_{\mathrm{air}}T_\infty\Delta p}{p_\infty}}. \]

Use \(R_{\mathrm{air}}=287\ \mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}\), unless the instructor supplies another value.

2. Pressure coefficient

\[ C_p=\frac{p-p_\infty}{q_\infty}. \]

Here \(p\) is the local surface pressure, \(p_\infty\) is the freestream static pressure, and \(q_\infty\) is dynamic pressure. Confirm the sign convention used by the supplied pressure files before plotting.

3. Sectional force coefficients

Using the pressure-port geometry and a documented trapezoidal integration,

\[ C_n=-\frac{1}{c}\oint C_p\,dy, \qquad C_a=\frac{1}{c}\oint C_p\,dz. \]

The discrete implementation is

\[ C_n=-\sum_i\frac{C_{p,i}+C_{p,i+1}}{2}\frac{\Delta y_i}{c}, \qquad C_a=\sum_i\frac{C_{p,i}+C_{p,i+1}}{2}\frac{\Delta z_i}{c}. \]

The lift and pressure-drag coefficients are

\[ C_l=C_n\cos\alpha-C_a\sin\alpha, \qquad C_d=C_n\sin\alpha+C_a\cos\alpha. \]

Use radians inside trigonometric functions.

4. Infinite-wing aerodynamic loading

For the infinite-wing baseline, calculate the sectional lift per unit span using

\[ \ell'_\infty=q_\infty c C_l. \]

Students test the infinite wing at multiple assigned values of \(\alpha\) and \(V_\infty\). Use those data to determine how \(C_l\), lift, and uncertainty change with the test condition.

5. Supplied finite-wing loading

The instructor supplies the finite-wing spanwise pressure data at one assigned angle of attack and airspeed. Calculate \(C_l(z)\) at the supplied span stations and convert it to spanwise lift-load intensity:

\[ w(z)=q_\infty c C_l(z). \]

Interpolate between the supplied stations using a documented method. Do not imply that the finite-wing dataset represents a full student-collected velocity or angle-of-attack survey.

6. Supplied tip-deflection model

For a cantilever of length \(L\), the supplied distributed-load model is

\[ \nu_{\mathrm{tip}} = \frac{1}{6EI}\int_0^L w(z)z^2(3L-z)\,dz. \]

For discrete spanwise stations, evaluate the integral with a documented numerical quadrature rule. If the instructor supplies an equivalent point load \(F_{\mathrm{eq}}\) acting at \(x_{\mathrm{eq}}\), the equivalent expression is

\[ \nu_{\mathrm{tip}} = \frac{F_{\mathrm{eq}}x_{\mathrm{eq}}^2(3L-x_{\mathrm{eq}})}{6EI}. \]

Use the supplied \(E\), \(I\), \(L\), geometry, and unit system. The structural parameters are instructor-provided inputs to the uncertainty model.

7. Condition-dependent uncertainty

The primary output is \(\nu_{\mathrm{tip}}\). The sweep variable is normally \(V_\infty\), with \(\alpha\) held at an assigned value or treated as a second approved condition variable. Propagate uncertainty from pressure, atmospheric pressure, temperature, airspeed, angle of attack, spanwise position, and instructor-designated structural parameters.

Report both

\[ \mathrm{SSU}_{\nu,\mathrm{sys}}(V_\infty)=b_{\nu,\mathrm{sys}}(V_\infty) \]

and

\[ \mathrm{NSU}_{\nu,\mathrm{sys}}(V_\infty) = \frac{b_{\nu,\mathrm{sys}}(V_\infty)}{|\nu_{\mathrm{tip}}(V_\infty)|}. \]

Use the normalized-sensitivity guide for the Taylor-series calculation and the same data-reduction model for Monte Carlo.

8. Comparison required for Tier 2

Compare the infinite-wing baseline and the supplied finite-wing result at the finite-wing condition. Explain how finite-wing spanwise information changes the predicted load and tip deflection, and state which result is appropriate for the client’s intended use.

Dataset 1 - Infinite-wing chord-wise pressure data

Source: PILOT low-speed wind-tunnel runs with the Clark Y-14 infinite-wing model.

Student collection: Students collect this dataset at multiple assigned angles of attack and freestream velocities.

Expected channels:

  • Differential pressure at the chord-wise surface ports.
  • Freestream or dynamic pressure.
  • Air temperature and atmospheric pressure.
  • Measured tunnel velocity.
  • Angle of attack and run identifier.

Expected format: LabVIEW-exported ASCII or CSV files with one file or clearly labeled block per test condition. The exact column order remains an instructor placeholder.

Expected outputs: \(C_p(x/c)\), \(C_n\), \(C_a\), \(C_l\), lift, residuals, and uncertainty versus velocity and angle of attack.

Source: Instructor-provided finite-wing spanwise pressure dataset.

Student collection: Students do not perform a full finite-wing sweep. The course provides one finite-wing dataset at a single assigned angle of attack and airspeed.

Expected channels:

  • Chord-wise pressure data at the supplied spanwise stations.
  • Spanwise station location \(z\).
  • Freestream or dynamic pressure.
  • Temperature, atmospheric pressure, and measured airspeed.
  • The supplied angle of attack and run identifier.

Expected outputs: \(C_l(z)\), lift-load intensity \(w(z)\), integrated lift, and finite-wing tip-deflection prediction at the supplied condition.

The finite-wing dataset is a validation and structural-loading input. Do not describe it as a student-collected multi-condition survey.

The instructor provides the structural equations and geometry needed to convert aerodynamic loading to tip deflection. The minimum inputs are \(E\), \(I\), beam length \(L\), chord \(c\), spanwise station locations, and any approved uncertainty estimates.

Students do not derive or redesign the whiffle tree for this Tier 2 branch.

Notes

Additional Notes - Aeroelasticity Tier 2

Pedagogical purpose

The branch connects a student-collected infinite-wing pressure survey to an instructor-provided finite-wing structural prediction. The main learning move is from pointwise pressure uncertainty to a condition-dependent recommendation about velocity and angle of attack.

Recommended emphasis

  • Treat the measured velocity and angle of attack as test conditions, not merely metadata.
  • Use the pressure measurement chain to explain why the uncertainty changes across the test matrix.
  • Use the infinite-wing result as the baseline model.
  • Use the finite-wing dataset at its one supplied condition to show why spanwise loading matters for tip deflection.
  • Keep measurement uncertainty, model-form discrepancy, and finite-wing data coverage separate.
  • Require both scaled output uncertainty and normalized uncertainty contributions.

Four-day planning note

Day 1 should be prediction and test-matrix planning. Day 2 should be the infinite-wing pressure survey. Day 3 should be data reduction, finite-wing processing, and uncertainty sweeps. Day 4 should be targeted validation and the client recommendation.

Instructor decisions still required

  • Which angles of attack and velocities are assigned?
  • Which infinite-wing pressure channels and sample-data columns are released?
  • Which single finite-wing angle of attack and airspeed are supplied?
  • What structural equations and geometry are released?
  • What uncertainty threshold defines an acceptable test condition?
  • Which sensor or setup improvements are students allowed to evaluate?

Interpretation prompt

Ask students whether a lower normalized uncertainty is always preferable. The intended answer is no: a condition also needs to be physically valid, safe, relevant to the client objective, and supported by an appropriate model.
