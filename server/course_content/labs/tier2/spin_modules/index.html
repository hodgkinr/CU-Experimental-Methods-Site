Spin Modules

30,000 Ft View

Students fit inertia and effective dynamic retardance from SC free-base records in which the base motor supplies torque. They use the fitted model to predict base angular acceleration under reaction-wheel torque and plot relative percent uncertainty versus RW torque. Existing RW records hold the base stationary and can characterize wheel torque/current and speed, but do not validate the free-base prediction. Overview

Overview - Spin Modules

In this Tier 2 investigation, students use existing SC (bare-spacecraft) free-base records to estimate inertia and effective dynamic retarding torque from base-motor support trials. They then use the SC fit to predict base angular acceleration for an RW torque input and plot predicted relative percent uncertainty against applied RW torque.

The model is a transfer prediction for a future free-base reaction-wheel experiment. Historical RW records hold the base stationary. They support RW torque/current and wheel-speed characterization, but do not measure free-base base acceleration and do not validate the prediction.

For moving motion, the simplified magnitude equation is

\[ |\alpha_{base}|=(|\tau_{RW}|-\tau_r)/(1000I_{fit}),\qquad |\tau_{RW}|>\tau_r. \]

Here torque is in mN\,m, inertia in kg\,m\(^2\), and acceleration in rad/s\(^2\); 1000 converts mN\,m to N\,m. Base acceleration is opposite the RW motor torque. The equation does not describe stationary breakaway. The effective retardance is fitted from moving SC support data; static breakaway is not measured.

The use of SC support-fit inertia and retardance for internal RW torque is an assumption. The supplied dynamics derivation separates base/spacecraft and wheel inertia, and available layout information does not establish the equivalence. Students should name this uncertainty and identify the measurements needed for a future validation: signed RW torque/current, wheel speed, base encoder rate, synchronized time, command transitions, and repeat trials.

The candidate region comes from a stated uncertainty decision rule. Meeting a selected “good” threshold is not experimental validation or universal safety. The working 3000 rpm wheel limit depends on torque, duration, and initial speed; it is not a torque ceiling. A gyro comparison and wheel-speed feasibility study are optional advanced extensions. A gyro may change empirical acceleration uncertainty but does not automatically change theoretical parameter/input uncertainty.

Unverified Derivation

Derivation: SC support fit and internal RW torque prediction

1. Fit the moving SC support response

Choose positive base rotation as the positive direction. While the base is moving, model constant retarding torque as a signed term opposite rotation:

\[ \tau_{base}=I\alpha_{base}+\tau_r\operatorname{sgn}(\omega_{base}). \]

For support trials in the positive direction this becomes \(\tau_{base}=I\alpha_{base}+\tau_r\). The available torque is expressed in mN\,m, while \(I\alpha\) is in N\,m, so the regression used for the records is

\[ \tau_{base,\mathrm{mN\,m}}=1000I_{fit}\alpha_{base}+\tau_r. \]

Here \(I_{fit}\) has units kg\,m\(^2\), \(\alpha\) has units rad/s\(^2\), and \(\tau_r\) is in mN\,m. Estimate \(\alpha\) as the slope of encoder angular rate in rad/s against recorded time in seconds over the steady command interval. Estimate support torque from baseline-corrected current and the base-motor torque constant. Use the five SC support levels and inspect the fit residuals; one record per level does not establish independent run-to-run repeatability.

The fitted \(\tau_r\) is an effective dynamic resisting torque for this support configuration. The data do not independently identify static breakaway torque, so the moving equation cannot determine whether a stationary base will start moving.

2. Transfer the torque source for a provisional prediction

For an internal reaction wheel, positive torque on the wheel produces an equal-and-opposite motor reaction torque on the base. With \(\tau_{RW}\) defined as wheel motor torque and \(\tau_{base}=-\tau_{RW}\), a provisional base equation is

\[ I_{base}\alpha_{base}+\tau_r\operatorname{sgn}(\omega_{base})=-\tau_{RW}. \]

For motion in the direction of the applied reaction torque, the acceleration magnitude is

\[ |\alpha_{base}|=\frac{|\tau_{RW}|-\tau_r}{1000I_{base}}, \qquad |\tau_{RW}|>\tau_r. \]

The predicted base acceleration points opposite the RW motor torque. Torque is in mN\,m, inertia in kg\,m\(^2\), and acceleration in rad/s\(^2\). The factor 1000 converts torque to N\,m. For \(|\tau_{RW}|\leq\tau_r\), do not extend this moving-branch formula: stationary friction and breakaway are not characterized.

3. Why the inertia transfer is not established

The supplied dynamics reference distinguishes spacecraft/base inertia \(I_s\) from wheel inertia \(I_w\) and gives

\[ I_s\dot\omega_s+I_w\dot\omega_w=0 \]

for negligible external torque. Thus internal RW motor torque transfers angular momentum between wheel and base. The available documentation does not demonstrate that the inertia estimated from a base-motor support trial equals the appropriate \(I_{base}\) for that internal-torque response: mounting, passive wheel motion, and what rotates with the support test have not been resolved from the layout. Accordingly, using \(I_{fit}\) in place of \(I_{base}\) is a provisional transfer assumption, not a derived identity.

The same caution applies to retardance: it is assumed that the effective moving resistance fitted in the SC support configuration is applicable to the later free-base RW configuration. A direct free-base RW torque-step experiment is needed to evaluate these assumptions.

4. Torque constants and uncertainty roles

The base motor constant converts SC support current to support torque; its uncertainty affects the fitted parameters. The RW motor constant converts RW motor current to the predicted input torque; its uncertainty affects the prediction. Never use one constant for both motors.

Manufacturer datasheets list nominal constants of 25.5 mN\,m/A for the base motor and 33.5 mN\,m/A for the RW motor. They do not state a ±0.05 mN\,m/A calibration tolerance. That bound is therefore a working uncertainty assumption if used in the class analysis, not a verified calibration uncertainty. Current-channel accuracy is not specified in the available sources; treating it as negligible is a first-pass assumption only.

For a first-pass prediction uncertainty, distinguish:

  • quantified support-fit inputs: acceleration-slope uncertainty from each SC record, the stated working uncertainty bound for \(K_{t,base}\), and support-fit residual scatter;
  • quantified prediction input: the stated working uncertainty bound for \(K_{t,RW}\) when torque is inferred from RW current, plus any explicitly defined prediction-discrepancy allowance;
  • assumptions: negligible current and DAQ/timing uncertainty;
  • unresolved terms: encoder quadrature/rate scale accuracy, current calibration, possible dependence among fit terms, configuration-transfer discrepancy, and independent trial repeatability.

Any combined standard uncertainty must state its distribution and independence assumptions. A residual-based discrepancy allowance is provisional; it is not a full characterization of model error.

5. Validation data needed

To test the transfer, run the base free while applying RW torque. Record signed RW current (or independently calibrated torque), RW speed, base encoder rate, synchronized timestamps, commanded torque transitions, and status/dropout flags. Repeat conditions, especially near the predicted retardance region. Predeclare the rate-fit window, estimate empirical acceleration and its uncertainty, and compare acceleration residuals against the SC-parameter prediction. The existing fixture-held RW files cannot perform this validation.

Working derivation. Confirm the rotating assembly and inertia definition with the hardware owner before representing the parameter transfer as established.

Spin Modules Tier 2: SC support fit and RW acceleration prediction

Scope and evidence boundary

The core analysis uses the five existing SC (bare-spacecraft) free-base support records. In those records the base motor supplies torque; students estimate acceleration from base encoder rate and fit inertia and effective dynamic retardance. The resulting model is then used to predict the base response to reaction-wheel (RW) torque in a future free-base test.

Historical RW records were collected with the base held stationary. They can characterize RW motor current/torque and wheel speed. They do not contain empirical free-base base acceleration and cannot validate the predicted RW-driven response.

Sign convention and moving branch

Let positive RW motor torque accelerate the wheel in the positive direction. Its reaction torque on the base has the opposite sign. Let \(\omega_{base}\) and \(\alpha_{base}\) be signed base rate and acceleration, \(\tau_r\geq0\) the magnitude of effective dynamic retardance, and \(I_{fit}\) the inertia estimated in the SC base-motor support configuration. On a moving branch, the signed support balance is

\[ \tau_{base}=1000I_{fit}\alpha_{base}+\tau_r\,\operatorname{sgn}(\omega_{base}), \]

where torque is in mN\,m, inertia in kg\,m\(^2\), and angular acceleration in rad/s\(^2\). For a future RW-driven base response, the assumed applied base torque is \(\tau_{base}=-\tau_{RW}\). In magnitude form,

\[ |\alpha_{base}|=\frac{|\tau_{RW}|-\tau_r}{1000I_{fit}}, \qquad |\tau_{RW}|>\tau_r. \]

The acceleration direction is opposite the RW motor torque direction. The factor 1000 converts mN\,m to N\,m. This equation applies only to the moving branch under the constant-retarding-torque approximation. At rest and at or below the fitted dynamic retardance, it does not predict whether or when motion begins; static breakaway was not separately measured.

Transfer assumption that remains open

The fitted parameter is an SC support-fit inertia, not an independently established base-only inertia for internal RW torque. The supplied dynamics derivation gives \(I_s\dot\omega_s+I_w\dot\omega_w=0\) when external torque is negligible and identifies wheel inertia separately. Depending on the actual mounting and support-test dynamics, the base motor test may identify a different effective inertia than the base response to internal RW torque. The available hardware layout does not resolve that equivalence. Carry \(I_{fit}\) into the RW prediction only as a stated provisional transfer assumption. A free-base RW torque-step test with measured current/torque, wheel speed, base encoder rate, synchronized timestamps, and repeated trials is needed to assess the transfer and prediction.

Measurement sequence and parameter roles

  1. For each SC support record at nominal 4, 5, 6, 8, and 10 mN\,m, estimate current zero from the pre-command interval and calculate support torque with the base-motor constant: \(\tau_{base}=K_{t,base}(I_{base}-I_0)\).
  2. Convert encoder rate from rpm to rad/s and fit rate against the recorded timestamps over the documented steady command interval. The slope estimates \(\alpha\); inspect gaps and fit residuals.
  3. Fit \(\tau_{base}=1000I_{fit}\alpha+\tau_r\) across the five trials. Interpret \(\tau_r\) as effective dynamic retardance, not independently measured static friction or breakaway torque.
  4. For the prediction, use RW torque magnitude. If that torque is inferred from current, use the RW-motor constant \(K_{t,RW}\), not the base-motor constant. Keep commanded torque and current-inferred torque distinct.
  5. Propagate support-slope uncertainty, base-motor constant uncertainty assumption, support-fit scatter, RW torque-input uncertainty, and any stated prediction-discrepancy term. Do not treat unresolved encoder, current, timing, or repeatability terms as measured values.

Uncertainty and decision rule

Plot predicted relative standard uncertainty \(100u_\alpha/|\alpha_{base}|\) in percent against assumed applied RW torque. Do not interpret relative uncertainty where the moving-branch predicted acceleration is zero or undefined. State the decision threshold used to mark a candidate region. A threshold such as 25% is a chosen analysis rule; it is not a hardware specification, proof of validation, or universal safety criterion. Predictions outside the observed SC support-torque span are extrapolations.

Separate (a) sources quantified in the calculation, (b) sources treated as negligible only under first-pass assumptions, and (c) unresolved sources. In the current working analysis, current-channel and DAQ/timing uncertainty are treated as negligible; encoder quadrature convention and processed-rate systematic accuracy, current calibration, and independent run-to-run repeatability remain unresolved.

Optional extensions

  • Wheel speed: The working 3000 rpm limit constrains torque together with command duration and initial wheel speed. It is not a universal torque ceiling. The historical fixture-held records can inform spin-up characterization but do not establish a free-base safe duration or envelope.
  • MEMS gyro: A gyro-based rate fit may change empirical acceleration uncertainty because sensor noise, bias, scale, filtering, timing, and calibration differ. Changing the empirical rate sensor does not automatically change the theoretical uncertainty curve from model parameters and torque input.

Spin-module model reference for Tier 2; values and transfer assumptions require instructor review before release.

Procedure

Procedure - Spin Modules Tier 2: SC support fit and RW prediction

Objective

Use existing SC free-base records to estimate inertia and effective dynamic retarding torque from base-motor support trials. Use those parameters, provisionally, to predict base angular acceleration versus reaction-wheel torque and plot predicted relative (percent) uncertainty. Historical RW records hold the base stationary; use them to characterize RW torque/current and wheel speed only. They do not provide free-base base acceleration or validate the prediction.

Before analysis or operation

Follow the instructor's power-up and wind-down procedure and the approved clamping and motor-command sequence. Confirm setup, channel labels, signs, units, and timestamp meaning. Preserve original files and document any processing. Use only instructor-approved limits. The current working limits are ±2 A motor current and 3000 rpm wheel speed; the latter is a speed limit, not a torque ceiling. No limit or safety margin should be inferred from the historical records.

Measurement and analysis sequence

A. SC base-motor support fit

  1. Load the SC records at nominal base-motor torque levels 4, 5, 6, 8, and 10 mN\,m. Keep the configuration label and original data unchanged.
  2. Identify time, base-motor command/current, and base encoder rate columns. Verify units and sign. Inspect timestamp spacing, gaps, clipping, and command transitions.
  3. Estimate base-current zero from the pre-command samples. Convert baseline-corrected current to support torque using the base-motor nominal torque constant, 25.5 mN\,m/A. A ±0.05 mN\,m/A uncertainty may be used only as a stated working assumption; the datasheet does not provide that tolerance. Current-channel accuracy is unresolved and is treated as negligible only for the supplied first-pass analysis.
  4. Convert encoder rate from rpm to rad/s. Fit rate against actual timestamps over the documented steady command interval (the solution reduction uses 1.5–5.5 s); the slope estimates angular acceleration. Report the fit interval, sample count, gaps, fit residuals, and the method used to estimate within-record slope uncertainty.
  5. Fit the moving support balance \(\tau_{base,\mathrm{mN\,m}}=1000I_{fit}\alpha+\tau_r\). Report units, residuals, and limitations. \(\tau_r\) is effective dynamic retardance; static breakaway is not separately measured.

B. Historical RW characterization

  1. Inspect the fixture-held RW records for command, RW current, and wheel speed. Keep commanded torque distinct from current-inferred torque \(\tau_{RW}=K_{t,RW}I_{RW}\). Use the RW-motor nominal constant, 33.5 mN\,m/A, not the base-motor value. Its ±0.05 mN\,m/A bound is a working uncertainty assumption, not a stated datasheet calibration tolerance.
  2. Report the observed current and speed range and check the working limits. State that fixture-held wheel-speed traces do not establish speed during a future free-base run; feasibility depends on applied torque, duration, and initial speed.

C. RW-input acceleration prediction and uncertainty

  1. Use the signed convention that RW motor torque on the wheel produces an equal-and-opposite reaction torque on the base. For the moving branch,

\[ |\alpha_{base}|=\frac{|\tau_{RW}|-\tau_r}{1000I_{fit}},\qquad |\tau_{RW}|>\tau_r. \]

Torque is mN\,m, inertia kg\,m\(^2\), and acceleration rad/s\(^2\); 1000 converts mN\,m to N\,m. Base acceleration is opposite the RW motor torque. At rest or at/below fitted dynamic retardance, the moving-branch expression does not establish breakaway or a stationary response.

  1. Make an RW torque sweep using the SC-fitted parameters and show which points are interpolation within the SC support torque range and which are extrapolation. Label the use of \(I_{fit}\) and \(\tau_r\) for RW torque as transfer assumptions. The supplied dynamics derivation treats wheel inertia separately in internal angular-momentum exchange, and available hardware documentation does not establish that the support-fit inertia is the correct base inertia for that transfer.
  2. Propagate the specified first-pass quantities: SC acceleration-slope uncertainty, base-motor torque-constant working bound in the parameter fit, support residual scatter, RW torque-constant working bound in the input torque, and any explicitly defined prediction-discrepancy term. State probability distributions, correlations/independence assumptions, and the method. Treat current and DAQ/timing uncertainty as negligible only under the supplied first-pass assumption. Do not invent accuracy values for the encoder or current channel.
  3. Produce the required plot of predicted relative standard uncertainty in base angular acceleration, percent, versus applied RW torque magnitude. Identify the moving branch and do not assign a relative percentage where the predicted acceleration is zero or undefined. Report component contributions or a source table; identify unresolved encoder scale/decoding systematic uncertainty, current calibration, transfer discrepancy, and lack of independent repeat runs.
  4. Apply the working decision rule “good” = predicted relative standard uncertainty ≤25% to select a candidate torque region, unless the instructor supplies a replacement rule. Explain that this chosen uncertainty cutoff does not prove free-base experimental validation, universal safety, or engineering acceptability for every use.

Future free-base validation data

The current analysis does not include a free-base RW acceleration observation. A later validation must apply measured RW torque with the base free and log signed RW current or independently measured torque, RW speed, base encoder rate, synchronized timestamps, command transitions, and status/dropout flags. Repeat trials, especially near the predicted retardance region. Predeclare the acceleration fit window, estimate acceleration and empirical uncertainty, and compare observations and residuals with the SC-based prediction. This tests the unresolved parameter transfer.

Optional advanced extensions

  • 3000 rpm wheel-speed comparison: Treat speed feasibility as a function of torque, duration, and initial speed, with instructor-set margin and sensor uncertainty when available. The rpm limit is not a universal torque ceiling.
  • MEMS gyro comparison: A calibrated gyro may change empirical acceleration uncertainty. It does not automatically change the theoretical model parameter/input uncertainty curve; only uncertainty entering parameter fitting or model inputs changes that curve.

Required products

  • SC trial table with current baseline, support torque, acceleration slope, slope uncertainty, fit window, and data-quality checks.
  • Support-fit parameter and residual summary, with inertia-transfer and dynamic-retardance assumptions labeled.
  • Historical RW torque/current and wheel-speed characterization, identified as fixture-held.
  • Predicted acceleration and uncertainty table, plus the required relative-percent uncertainty plot versus RW torque.
  • Decision rule and candidate torque region, uncertainty-source discussion, and explicit limitations.
  • List of measurements needed for a future free-base RW validation.

FAQs

FAQs - Spin Modules Tier 2

What is the core task?

Use the existing SC free-base records to fit inertia and effective dynamic retardance from base-motor support torque. Apply that fit provisionally to predict base angular acceleration versus reaction-wheel torque. Propagate the stated first-pass uncertainties and produce the required plot of predicted relative percent uncertainty in acceleration versus RW torque.

Do the supplied RW records measure base acceleration?

No. The historical RW records hold the base stationary. They can support RW torque/current and wheel-speed characterization, but they do not measure a free-base acceleration response and cannot validate the model prediction.

What equation should I use?

For the moving branch, use

\[ |\alpha_{base}|=(|\tau_{RW}|-\tau_r)/(1000I_{fit}),\quad |\tau_{RW}|>\tau_r. \]

Torque is in mN\,m, inertia in kg\,m\(^2\), and acceleration in rad/s\(^2\). Base acceleration is opposite the RW motor torque; 1000 converts mN\,m to N\,m. At rest or at/below fitted dynamic retardance, this equation does not establish breakaway. Static breakaway is not independently measured.

Why is using the fitted inertia an assumption?

The support fit comes from a base-motor test, while RW motor torque is internal and exchanges angular momentum between the wheel and base. The dynamics derivation treats wheel inertia separately, and the available hardware layout does not prove that the support-fit inertia is the correct base inertia for the RW response. State this transfer assumption and explain how a free-base RW experiment could test it.

Which torque constant applies?

Use the base-motor constant only to infer SC support torque and fit parameters. Use the RW-motor constant only to infer the RW torque input. Datasheets list nominal constants of 25.5 and 33.5 mN\,m/A, respectively, but do not specify the ±0.05 mN\,m/A bounds used in the working analysis. Those bounds are assumptions, not verified calibration uncertainties.

What uncertainty sources belong in the first-pass curve?

The working analysis includes SC acceleration-slope uncertainty, the assumed base-motor torque-constant bound, SC fit residual scatter, the assumed RW torque-constant bound, and a provisional prediction-discrepancy term. Current and timing uncertainty are treated as negligible only by explicit first-pass assumption. Encoder scale/decoding accuracy, current calibration, between-run repeatability, and transfer discrepancy remain unresolved. Do not invent values for them.

What is a “good” torque region?

Use the working rule “good” = predicted relative standard uncertainty ≤25%, unless the instructor supplies another threshold. The cutoff is a chosen analysis criterion, not proof of experimental validation, universal safety, or suitability for every client application. Predictions outside the SC support-torque range are extrapolations.

What data would validate the model later?

Apply measured RW torque with the base free and record signed RW current or independently measured torque, wheel speed, base encoder rate, synchronized timestamps, command changes, and status/dropout flags. Repeat trials, predeclare the acceleration fit window, and compare measured acceleration and residuals with the prediction.

Are the 3000 rpm limit and MEMS gyro required?

No. Both are optional advanced extensions. Wheel-speed feasibility depends on applied torque, command duration, and initial speed; 3000 rpm is not a universal torque ceiling. A gyro may change empirical acceleration uncertainty through its own noise, bias, scale, and calibration, but it does not automatically change the model's theoretical parameter/input uncertainty curve.

Is angular momentum capacity or controller design required?

No. Angular momentum capacity, saturation-time analysis, panel comparison, and controller design are outside the bare-minimum scope unless assigned separately.
