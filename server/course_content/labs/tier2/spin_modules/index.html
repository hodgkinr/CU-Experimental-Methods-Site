Spin Modules

Evaluate gyro calibration, reaction-wheel and spacecraft-body inertia estimates, coast-down resistance, and rotational-response uncertainty across useful angular-rate and applied-torque conditions.

30,000 Ft View

In this experiment you will use the CU Boulder spin module to calibrate a rate gyro and estimate moments of inertia from rotational motion data. The core deliverables are the reaction wheel moment of inertia and the spacecraft body moment of inertia using encoder measurements and gyro calibration information where assigned. Comparisons to theoretical/reference values for the brass reaction wheel, motor contribution, SolidWorks spacecraft model, and possible aluminum plate configuration are placeholders until the teaching team confirms the hardware files and reference values.

Tier 2 Elevator Pitch

Students act as consultants evaluating whether the spin module can measure and predict rotational behavior reliably across a range of rates and applied torques. They calibrate the gyro, estimate both reaction-wheel and spacecraft-body inertia, characterize resistance, map uncertainty against the relevant test condition, and recommend where to test and what hardware or measurement change would improve the experiment.

Overview

Overview - Spin Modules

In this lab, you will work with a single-axis spacecraft mockup called the CU Boulder Spin Module. The experiment focuses on sensor calibration and inertia estimation rather than closed-loop attitude control.

The student-facing core of this lab is:

  • calibrate the MEMS rate gyro using encoder data as the truth reference
  • estimate the reaction wheel moment of inertia from a constant-torque spin-up test
  • estimate the spacecraft body moment of inertia from rotational acceleration data
  • compare experimental moment-of-inertia estimates to theoretical or reference values when those values are provided

By the end of the lab, you should be able to:

  • identify the measurement chain connecting the gyro, encoder, Hall sensors, current sensors, myRIO, Arduino DUE, Xbee link, and LabVIEW VI
  • fit gyro bias and scale factor from calibration data
  • use angular velocity data to estimate angular acceleration
  • compute moment of inertia from torque and angular acceleration
  • explain how calibration, sensor choice, and missing reference values affect confidence in the final estimate

Theoretical Framework - Spin Modules Tier 2 Experiment

1. Gyro calibration

Use the encoder as the angular-rate reference and model the raw gyro output as

\[ \omega_{\mathrm{gyro}}=K\omega_{\mathrm{true}}+b+\epsilon_{\mathrm{gyro}}. \]

The calibrated rate is

\[ \omega_{\mathrm{calibrated}}=\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{gyro}}-b}{K}. \]

Fit \(K\) and \(b\) for each assigned calibration run, compare the fitted parameters, and report the uncertainty in the calibration.

2. Inertia from spin-up

For the reaction-wheel test, hold the spacecraft body stationary and apply torque to the reaction wheel. For the body-inertia test, allow the spacecraft to rotate and apply torque with the base motor. In either case, the initial model is

\[ I\dot{\omega}=\tau. \]

Use

\[ \tau=k_t I_{\mathrm{motor}}, \qquad I=\frac{\tau}{\dot{\omega}}. \]

Fit \(\dot{\omega}\) from the rate history rather than differentiating noisy samples directly. Report the individual-trial estimates, mean, scatter, and uncertainty.

3. Measurement-chain interpretation

The torque estimate depends on the motor torque constant \(k_t\) and current measurement. The angular-acceleration estimate depends on the selected encoder or Hall-sensor channel, the sampling rate, the fitting window, and the rate calibration. Each of these quantities belongs in the uncertainty model.

4. Resistance model

Use the lumped viscous-plus-Coulomb model

\[ I\dot{\omega}+c\omega+\tau_0\operatorname{sgn}(\omega)=\tau_{\mathrm{applied}}. \]

For a coast-down record, \(\tau_{\mathrm{applied}}=0\), so

\[ \dot{\omega} = -\frac{c\omega+\tau_0\operatorname{sgn}(\omega)}{I}. \]

Estimate \(c\) and \(\tau_0\) from the coast-down data or use instructor-provided values. Keep the two parameters in LaTeX notation in plots, tables, and prose.

5. Dynamic prediction

For a selected applied torque and angular rate,

\[ \dot{\omega}(\tau_{\mathrm{applied}},\omega) = \frac{\tau_{\mathrm{applied}}-c\omega-\tau_0\operatorname{sgn}(\omega)}{I}. \]

Integrate this equation when a predicted rate history is required. Compare the prediction with measured rate and acceleration, and distinguish sensor uncertainty from an imperfect resistance model.

6. Tier 2 uncertainty envelope

Use applied torque as the primary condition for the spin-up and inertia studies. Use angular rate as the primary condition for gyro calibration and coast-down analysis. For any derived output \(y\), report

\[ \mathrm{SSU}_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q)=b_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q) \]

and

\[ \mathrm{NSU}_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q)=\frac{b_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q)}{|y(q)|}, \]

where \(q\) is the selected torque or rate condition. Use both Taylor-series sensitivity coefficients and Monte Carlo propagation at representative conditions.

Model Equations Reference - Spin Modules Tier 2 Experiment

This reference lists the equations needed for gyro calibration, inertia estimation, resistance characterization, rotational prediction, and uncertainty propagation.

1. Gyro calibration

Model the raw gyro output as

\[ \omega_{\mathrm{gyro}}=K\omega_{\mathrm{true}}+b, \]

where \(K\) is the scale factor and \(b\) is the bias. With the encoder as the reference,

\[ \omega_{\mathrm{calibrated}}=\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{gyro}}-b}{K}. \]

Fit \(K\) and \(b\) from the assigned calibration data and report their uncertainty.

2. Angular position from rate

\[ \theta(t)=\theta_0+\int_{t_0}^{t}\omega(\tau)\,d\tau. \]

In MATLAB, the cumtrapz function is an appropriate numerical integration method. Compare integrated calibrated gyro rate with encoder angle and report drift.

3. Motor torque

When torque is inferred from motor current,

\[ \tau=k_t I_{\mathrm{motor}}, \]

where \(k_t\) is the motor torque constant. Use separate instructor-provided values for the reaction-wheel motor and base motor.

4. Reaction-wheel inertia

For a reaction-wheel spin-up test with the spacecraft body held stationary,

\[ I_{\mathrm{RW}}\dot{\omega}_{\mathrm{RW}}=\tau_{\mathrm{RW}}. \]

The initial estimate is

\[ I_{\mathrm{RW}}=\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{RW}}}{\dot{\omega}_{\mathrm{RW}}}. \]

Use multiple torque trials, fit angular acceleration, and report the mean, scatter, and propagated uncertainty.

5. Spacecraft-body inertia

For a base-motor spin-up test with the spacecraft allowed to rotate,

\[ I_{\mathrm{sc}}\dot{\omega}_{\mathrm{sc}}=\tau_{\mathrm{base}}, \]

for the initial model. Use the full resistance model below when the coast-down analysis shows that resistance is not negligible.

\[ I_{\mathrm{sc}}=\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{base}}}{\dot{\omega}_{\mathrm{sc}}}. \]

6. Coast-down resistance model

Use the lumped viscous-plus-Coulomb resistance model

\[ I\dot{\omega}+c\omega+\tau_0\operatorname{sgn}(\omega)=\tau_{\mathrm{applied}}. \]

Here \(c\) is the viscous resistance coefficient and \(\tau_0\) is the approximately constant Coulomb-friction torque. During coast-down, \(\tau_{\mathrm{applied}}=0\), so

\[ \dot{\omega} = -\frac{c\omega+\tau_0\operatorname{sgn}(\omega)}{I}. \]

Estimate \(c\) and \(\tau_0\) from the supplied coast-down data or use instructor-provided estimates. State clearly whether the values are measured, fitted, or supplied.

7. Rotational-performance prediction

For a specified applied torque and angular rate,

\[ \dot{\omega} = \frac{\tau_{\mathrm{applied}}-c\omega-\tau_0\operatorname{sgn}(\omega)}{I}. \]

Use this equation to predict angular acceleration or integrate the predicted rate history. Compare the prediction with measured data.

8. Tier 2 uncertainty targets

For gyro calibration, evaluate uncertainty in \(\omega_{\mathrm{calibrated}}\) versus encoder angular rate. For inertia estimation, evaluate uncertainty in \(I=\tau/\dot{\omega}\) versus applied torque. For rotational-performance prediction, evaluate uncertainty in \(\dot{\omega}\) or predicted rate history versus applied torque and angular rate.

Report

\[ \mathrm{SSU}_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q)=b_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q) \]

and

\[ \mathrm{NSU}_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q)=\frac{b_{y,\mathrm{sys}}(q)}{|y(q)|}, \]

where \(q\) is the selected torque or rate test condition. Preserve covariance among parameters estimated from the same regression when a covariance matrix is available.

Part 1 — Spin Module Power-Up Procedure

Primary source: ASEN3801 Lab 3, Setup Procedure (pages 12–13); supplemental: ASEN3200 LabA1 (pages 8–9).

  1. Use the clamp provided to secure the spin module base to the lab station top. Place the clamp on the base plate at the spot labeled "clamp here".
  2. CHECK that you have completed step 1. If you do not clamp the spin module down and you run the motor, it will fly off the table and be damaged.
  3. Connect the USB cable Type B end to the National Instruments myRIO port labeled "DEVICE" (the middle port). The other Type A end connects to the lab station computer. (Do not connect to the Type A top port on the myRIO.)
  4. Connect the power adapter to the barrel connector. The power light on the myRIO should now be illuminated blue.
  5. Wait 1–2 minutes until the Status light on the myRIO turns from orange to off.
  6. Verify the lab assistant has connected the second power supply to the base station printed circuit board (PCB). Verify the U8002A power supply is set to a limit of 12.00 V and 0.300 A (press "Display Limit" on the supply). Do not plug in any power connections until power supply settings have been verified.
  7. Confirm that the satellite 9V battery pack has been plugged in. Wait for the lower board motor controller to flash its green LED.
  8. Once the above steps are complete, turn on the satellite board power located on the top PCB, inside the spin module chassis, using the red toggle switch.
  9. Verify the board goes through a series of status LEDs shown near the toggle switch. The remaining LED under the switch should be Yellow; the Alive LED should be green.
  10. Open All_Spin_Module_Labs.vi located in your current semester's class folder (Courses H:/AES/Software/VIs/ASEN 3200/). Do NOT run the VI yet.
  11. Check the battery voltage meter in the VI status panel (bottom right corner). If the battery voltage of the satellite's onboard battery pack drops below 14 V, turn off the top satellite board with the red toggle switch and request staff assistance for new batteries.
  12. The "Reinitialize Arduino" button is used if you notice the gyro voltage drift over time from zero at rest. Use it to reset this offset bias.
  13. The green indicators show if the myRIO (on the base) and the Arduino DUE (inside the satellite) are properly connected and communicating.
  14. Check the "Link Speed" dial — a good typical value is 1.5 or higher. If you see frequent dropouts to zero, the Xbee channel may be experiencing interference from other wireless sources, or the battery voltage may be too low.

NOTE: For safety, you cannot change the LabVIEW operating modes (Torque vs. Gyro) of the Spin Module while the VI is running.

Part 2 — Rate Gyro Calibration

Primary source: ASEN3801 Section 3.1; supplemental: ASEN3200 LabA1 Section 5.1.

Purpose: Characterize the MEMS gyro bias \(b\) and scale factor \(K\) relative to the encoder truth reference.

Sub-Part 2a: Manual Experiment (Qualitative)

  1. Select the "Gyro" tab on the VI front panel.
  2. Start running the VI by clicking "play". The VI will now display live gyro measurements.
  3. Select "Manual Control" from the VI front panel.
  4. Move the spacecraft gently back and forth by hand in a smooth, sinusoidal manner so that a good linear relationship between gyro rate measurement and input angular rate can be observed on the VI.
  • Note: Abrupt changes in angular velocity lead to non-linear effects in the gyro output that appear as "looping" in the relationship between true angular rate and gyro measurement. Use gentle, sinusoidal motion to avoid this.
  1. Observe how moving the spacecraft at different rates affects the data collection.
  2. Collect approximately 1 minute of data for observation; there is no need to save this file.
  3. Take notes on the performance of the MEMS gyro for later discussion.

Sub-Part 2b: Motor-Driven Calibration Data Collection

  1. While still on the "Gyro" tab, select "Automatic RW Control" from the VI front panel.
  2. Set the desired output frequency and amplitude. A 0.2 Hz sine wave at an amplitude of 0.5 Amps is a good starting point.
  3. Click "Begin Test". The VI will display data on the screen (not yet logging to file).
  4. Collect approximately 1 minute of data.
  5. Click "Write to File" and save using the naming convention:

YEAR_MONTH_DAY_SEC_MEMS_F#C#

  • Example: 2024_XX_XX_001_MEMS_F02C05 (Section 001, 0.2 Hz, 0.5 A)
  1. Repeat for at least 2 more different frequency/amplitude combinations, saving each file.
  2. Use the "STOP" button when done collecting.

Data quality note: If "DUMPING" is displayed and illuminated (rather than "QUEUING"), data will not be saved — check this before clicking Write to File.

Sub-Part 2c: MATLAB Analysis

From ASEN3801 Section 3.1, Tasks 1–4.

  1. For one data set: import the data into MATLAB. Identify what information is in each column and convert the rate data from RPM to rad/s.
  2. On the same graph, plot the time history of the encoder rate measurement (true angular rate) and the gyro rate measurement (in rad/s). Note: you should observe that the gyro rate measurements have the opposite sign from the encoder rate measurements.
  3. To determine the calibration parameters, plot the encoder rate measurement on the horizontal axis and the gyro rate measurement on the vertical axis. Fit a line to the data to determine:
  • Gyro bias \(b\) [rad/s] (y-intercept)
  • Scale factor \(K\) [unitless, (rad/s)/(rad/s)] (slope)
  1. Write a calibration equation for how the gyro rate measurements should be corrected. Apply the calibration to the data set and plot the time history of encoder rate and calibrated rate on the same axes — they should match well.
  2. Make a table of b and K for each run. Compute the mean and standard deviation of b and K across all runs. Explain which trials you used and which portions of the data, if any, you removed before computing statistics.
  3. For two data sets, compute and plot:
  • (a) Time history of encoder rate and calibrated rate
  • (b) Time history of angular rate error (calibrated rate − encoder rate)
  • (c) Time history of true angular position (from encoder) and measured angular position (from calibrated gyro rate); compute positions by integrating the respective rates.
  • (d) Time history of angular position error (measured − true)
  • (e) Angular position error as a function of encoder rate (encoder rate on x-axis)

Tier 2 Scope and Four-Day Execution Plan

The Tier 2 spin-module branch is a consulting-style evaluation of the rotational measurement system. The purpose is to determine which rate and torque conditions provide useful parameter estimates and dynamic predictions.

Day 1 - Prediction and calibration

  • inspect the gyro, encoder, Hall-sensor, current, motor, DAQ, and wireless measurement chain;
  • verify units, limits, and available torque constants;
  • collect or analyze gyro calibration data;
  • commit to the initial rate and torque test matrix;
  • predict the initial uncertainty envelope.

Day 2 - Spin-up and inertia data

  • collect multiple reaction-wheel torque trials;
  • collect multiple base-motor torque trials for spacecraft-body response;
  • record actual current, rate, torque command, and timing information;
  • preserve raw data and document battery, link, clamp, and operating-limit status.

Day 3 - Resistance and uncertainty analysis

  • collect or analyze a coast-down segment;
  • estimate \(c\) and \(\tau_0\) as lumped resistance parameters, or use instructor-provided values;
  • estimate reaction-wheel and spacecraft-body inertia;
  • run the Taylor and Monte Carlo uncertainty sweeps versus torque and/or rate;
  • identify candidate useful and inappropriate regions.

Day 4 - Targeted validation and recommendation

  • repeat selected torque or rate conditions near an uncertainty-envelope boundary;
  • compare predicted and measured angular response;
  • quantify one setup change, such as improved current/torque resolution, gyro calibration,

sampling, or an additional resistance measurement;

  • finalize the client recommendation.

Scope boundary

The minimum Tier 2 result is not a complete spacecraft controls study. Reaction-wheel saturation time, solar-panel comparison, and controller design are enrichment topics unless the instructor assigns them. The required result is a defensible uncertainty envelope and test recommendation based on gyro calibration, both inertia estimates, and a resistance characterization.

FAQs - Spin Modules

What are the core deliverables for this lab?

Determine the reaction wheel moment of inertia and the spacecraft body moment of inertia. The gyro calibration is also part of the core workflow because it explains how the rate sensor compares to the encoder reference.

Are angular momentum capacity and saturation time required?

They are optional enrichment calculations unless your instructor explicitly assigns them. The core Tier 2 result is the uncertainty envelope and test recommendation.

Which sensor is the truth reference for gyro calibration?

The existing source notes use the encoder as the truth reference for angular rate during gyro calibration.

What motor constants do I need?

The reaction wheel and base motor torque constants are needed to convert current to torque. They are currently marked as missing in the instrumentation notes.

What changes for Tier 2?

The calibration and inertia calculations remain, but they are now used to evaluate a condition-dependent uncertainty envelope. The group must recommend useful and inappropriate angular-rate or applied-torque conditions and propose one setup change that would move the useful region.

Should the uncertainty plot use angular rate or torque?

Use angular rate for gyro calibration and coast-down analysis. Use applied torque or measured motor current for spin-up and inertia testing because that is the controlled test input.

Is the full resistance model required?

The Tier 2 prediction should use the lumped model

\[ I\dot{\omega}+c\omega+\tau_0\operatorname{sgn}(\omega)=\tau_{\mathrm{applied}}. \]

If the group cannot collect a coast-down dataset, the instructor may provide \(c\) and \(\tau_0\), but the group must identify them as provided parameters and include their uncertainty or limitation in the report.

What is outside the four-day scope?

Complete controller design, solar-panel comparison, saturation-time analysis, and detailed hardware redesign are enrichment topics unless explicitly assigned.
