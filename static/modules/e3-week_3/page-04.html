V&V Plan and Oral Defense Worksheet

A reading-format worksheet that helps students convert the W13 V&V plan lectures into a complete written plan and oral defense preparation artifact. It maps each required V&V plan section to its evidence requirement, lists common failure modes, and provides skeptical oral-defense questions covering model claim, intended use, response features, validation metrics, calibration versus validation, uncertainty adequacy, DAQ/sampling, residual patterns, feasibility, safety, CLO 6, and AI-assisted claim verification.

9 min Readings

Learning Objectives

  • Select sensors for an original aerospace experiment with written rationale connecting sensor specifications to accuracy requirements and intended use — CLO 1
  • Specify DAQ configuration and signal conditioning strategy for an original experiment, including sampling rate justified by Nyquist argument and anti-aliasing filter cutoff justified relative to signal bandwidth — CLO 1, CLO 4
  • Use MATLAB-based uncertainty analysis to predict the measurement performance of a proposed sensor and DAQ configuration before any hardware is selected — CLO 2
  • Investigate at least one sensor or measurement platform not provided in the course and evaluate whether it offers meaningful advantages for a specific measurement problem — CLO 6

Integrating the Proposal and Preparing for the Oral Defense

Review the design as one engineering argument

Use this guide to refine the existing proposal and prepare for its scheduled oral defense. It is not a separate worksheet or submission.

Proposal coherence check

Read the proposal once as a technical reviewer. Can you follow this chain?

> Question and intended use → model and expected response → experimental conditions → measurement method → uncertainty and limitations → comparison and conclusion

If a section does not support this chain, shorten it or explain why it is needed. Do not add a section merely to fill out a standard document structure.

Check that:

  • the proposed experiment could measure the response the model predicts;
  • instrument, sampling, conditioning, and DAQ choices are relevant to the expected signal;
  • uncertainty and practical limitations are connected to what can be concluded;
  • the comparison criterion is meaningful for the intended use;
  • the outside source or method informed a real choice; and
  • technical claims and specifications have credible sources.

Oral defense preparation

Prepare to explain the design and answer questions about:

  1. why this question and expected response matter;
  2. why the proposed conditions and measurements can answer the question;
  3. which measurement, sampling, or conditioning choice matters most;
  4. what uncertainty or limitation could change the conclusion;
  5. what outcome would support or challenge the model; and
  6. what alternative or outside resource influenced the decision.

The defense is a conversation about the team’s proposal. It is not a request to repeat the document or add new analysis.
