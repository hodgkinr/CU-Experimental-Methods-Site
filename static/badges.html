Aerospace Experimental Methods

Badges

Each badge is tied to one Course Learning Outcome. The assessments below are the major summative places where evidence for that badge can be produced and defended.

How the badge states work

Each Course Learning Outcome contains several sub-CLOs. A student earns the parent badge only after demonstrating every sub-CLO at the Proficient level.

  • Proficient: The student can perform every part of the outcome at the acceptable ASEN 3501 standard and support the work with defensible evidence. The black-and-white badge represents this state.
  • Mastery: The student can perform every part of the outcome with depth, independence, consistency, integration, and engineering judgment. The color badge represents this state.

CLO 1 Mastery Badge

Design and conduct experiments

Design and conduct aerospace experiments by applying measurement fundamentals, instrumentation principles, and best practices in small team environments.

What each sub-CLO requires

  • CLO1.1 — Design by Applying Measurement Fundamentals

    Proficient: Identify the quantity, variables, range, units, procedure, controls, and confounders needed to answer the question.
    Mastery: Anticipate how measurement limits and practical constraints affect the claim, then make traceable design tradeoffs.

  • CLO1.2 — Conduct by Applying Measurement Fundamentals

    Proficient: Execute the procedure, record usable measurements with units and context, take justified observations, and identify important variation.
    Mastery: Work independently, diagnose anomalies, document decisions, and preserve a record another engineer can reconstruct.

  • CLO1.3 — Design by Applying Instrumentation Principles

    Proficient: Select instrumentation that matches the measurand, range, accuracy, bandwidth, and setup.
    Mastery: Compare plausible options against uncertainty, installation effects, risks, and practical constraints.

  • CLO1.4 — Conduct by Applying Instrumentation Principles

    Proficient: Configure and use instruments correctly, interpret their outputs, and apply specifications or calibration information.
    Mastery: Diagnose instrument and installation artifacts, compare measurement paths, and justify corrective action.

  • CLO1.5 — Small-Team Best Practices

    Proficient: Prepare, contribute reliably, communicate, maintain records, meet handoffs, and respond to feedback.
    Mastery: Anticipate dependencies, resolve technical or coordination problems, improve the team process, and preserve individual accountability.

Major summative assessment opportunities

  • Lab 1: Power, Voltage, and the Language of UncertaintyWeeks 2-5

    Students conduct a controlled bench experiment using a power supply, resistor, and multimeter while documenting proper measurement practice. The simple circuit keeps the focus on careful execution and defensible measurement records.

  • Tier 1: Forward Analysis, Reverse Prediction, and Combined SynthesisWeeks 5-8

    Students execute assigned experiments, identify variables and confounders, record measurements with uncertainty, and compare sensor methods. The assignment moves from following a procedure toward disciplined experimental conduct.

  • Tier 2: Predict, Execute, Analyze, ImproveWeeks 8-11

    Groups prepare, run, and document a more complex aerospace experiment under practical lab constraints. They must record calibration status, anomalies, setup decisions, and evidence while the experiment is happening.

  • Final Lab: Paper Experimental Design and V&V PlanWeeks 12-15

    Students design a feasible experiment with variables, controls, confounding factors, safety considerations, and a bounded test matrix. The assignment evaluates whether their planned procedure could be executed by an engineering team.

CLO 2 Mastery Badge

Apply uncertainty and statistics

Apply statistical methods and uncertainty analysis to quantify experimental error and establish confidence in results.

What each sub-CLO requires

  • CLO2.1 — Quantify Experimental Uncertainty

    Proficient: Identify relevant inputs, apply a suitable propagation method, and report an estimate with units and a clear uncertainty statement.
    Mastery: Use and compare partial-derivative propagation and Monte Carlo when appropriate, state assumptions, identify dominant sources, and use the analysis to guide decisions.

  • CLO2.2 — Establish Confidence in Results

    Proficient: Compare a result with a prediction, method, or requirement using an explicit criterion and appropriate interval or coverage language.
    Mastery: Integrate uncertainty, validation metrics, residuals, calibration, model assumptions, and intended use into a precise conclusion and next action.

Major summative assessment opportunities

  • Lab 1: Power, Voltage, and the Language of UncertaintyWeeks 2-5

    A central objective of this assignment is to quantify uncertainty in power estimates using datasheet-based Type B inputs, first-order propagation, and Monte Carlo simulation. Students must state estimates with units, interval type, method, and coverage basis.

  • Tier 1: Forward Analysis, Reverse Prediction, and Combined SynthesisWeeks 5-8

    Students propagate uncertainty for derived quantities, rank dominant error sources, and use sensor specifications before seeing data. The work reinforces uncertainty as part of the argument rather than a calculation appended at the end.

  • Tier 2: Predict, Execute, Analyze, ImproveWeeks 8-11

    Students estimate uncertainty before hardware access and then revisit the uncertainty budget after collecting data. They must decide whether the measurement chain can resolve the intended quantity and identify dominant uncertainty sources.

  • Final Lab: Paper Experimental Design and V&V PlanWeeks 12-15

    Students perform prospective uncertainty analysis before any data exists and judge whether the expected measurement performance is adequate. The uncertainty work informs design decisions rather than merely describing past measurements.

CLO 3 Mastery Badge

Integrate models and data

Integrate theoretical models with experimental data to validate and evaluate aerospace system behavior.

What each sub-CLO requires

  • CLO3.1 — Validate Models with Data

    Proficient: Align the model and measured quantity, define a comparison metric, compare them quantitatively, and distinguish validation from calibration.
    Mastery: Test assumptions, interpret residual structure, separate measurement effects from model-form error, and propose specific changes supported by the discrepancy.

  • CLO3.2 — Evaluate Aerospace System Behavior

    Proficient: Use model and data together to explain relevant trends, response features, or parameter effects.
    Mastery: Integrate multiple conditions or measurement channels, weigh competing explanations, identify transfer limits, and make a clear engineering judgment.

Major summative assessment opportunities

  • Tier 1: Forward Analysis, Reverse Prediction, and Combined SynthesisWeeks 5-8

    The assignment asks students to compare measured data to predictive models and interpret discrepancies using named validation metrics. The blind prediction phase makes the model-data relationship explicit before results are available.

  • Tier 2: Predict, Execute, Analyze, ImproveWeeks 8-11

    The committed prediction, acceptance criterion, residual analysis, and improvement proposal all connect model behavior to measured evidence. Students learn to distinguish ordinary scatter from model-form discrepancy or setup effects.

  • Final Lab: Paper Experimental Design and V&V PlanWeeks 12-15

    The final plan must define a predictive model claim, validation metrics, acceptance criteria, and expected response features. Students explain how future data would inform, challenge, or improve the model.

CLO 4 Mastery Badge

Implement signal and DAQ methods

Implement signal conditioning, sampling, and data acquisition techniques to ensure accurate experimental measurements.

What each sub-CLO requires

  • CLO4.1 — Signal Conditioning

    Proficient: Explain how gain, filtering, noise, and anti-aliasing affect measurement quality and select a reasonable approach.
    Mastery: Design or evaluate the signal chain across bandwidth, dynamic range, noise, filtering, gain, and transient tradeoffs.

  • CLO4.2 — Sampling

    Proficient: Use the relevant highest frequency to justify the sample rate, explain aliasing, and identify the role of the anti-aliasing filter.
    Mastery: Account for bandwidth, transients, filter roll-off, timing, resolution, storage, and hardware limits using appropriate evidence.

  • CLO4.3 — Data Acquisition

    Proficient: Configure or specify range, resolution, channels, sample rate, triggering or synchronization, and calibration status to match the signal.
    Mastery: Evaluate the complete acquisition chain, diagnose artifacts, and trace DAQ choices into the uncertainty and validation argument.

Major summative assessment opportunities

  • Tier 2: Predict, Execute, Analyze, ImproveWeeks 8-11

    Tier 2 makes sensor range, DAQ configuration, calibration status, and measurement-chain adequacy part of the central assignment. Students must show that instrumentation choices can support the intended validation claim.

  • Final Lab: Paper Experimental Design and V&V PlanWeeks 12-15

    Students specify the full measurement chain, including sensors, signal conditioning, sampling rate, DAQ range and resolution, filtering, and installation effects. These choices must be justified using expected signal levels and practical constraints.

CLO 5 Mastery Badge

Communicate results

Communicate experimental results effectively through technical reports and presentations, with clear description of methods, results, and limitations.

What each sub-CLO requires

  • CLO5.1 — Written Communication

    Proficient: Communicate methods, results, limitations, and conclusions with traceable figures, tables, labels, units, and calculations. Quantitative claims include an estimate, uncertainty, and uncertainty technique when applicable.
    Mastery: Build a concise technical argument with visible assumptions, evidence-based figures, calibrated confidence language, and meaningful limitations.

  • CLO5.2 — Oral Communication

    Proficient: Explain the experiment, methods, results, limitations, and conclusions clearly and answer reasonable questions accurately.
    Mastery: Defend the work under questioning, adapt to the audience, connect answers across CLOs, and respond to challenges with evidence and judgment.

Major summative assessment opportunities

  • Lab 1: Power, Voltage, and the Language of UncertaintyWeeks 2-5

    The brief report and oral interview require students to explain what they measured, what uncertainty means, and what limitations remain. This directly practices concise technical communication about experimental claims.

  • Tier 1: Forward Analysis, Reverse Prediction, and Combined SynthesisWeeks 5-8

    Students communicate their analysis through an individual report, a prediction memo, and a combined technical presentation. The paired synthesis requires them to explain what predictions got right, what they missed, and why.

  • Tier 2: Predict, Execute, Analyze, ImproveWeeks 8-11

    The group report, quad chart, Q&A, and peer evaluations require compact, defensible communication of prediction-to-outcome logic. Students practice defending conclusions by tracing evidence rather than restating claims.

  • Final Lab: Paper Experimental Design and V&V PlanWeeks 12-15

    Students produce written design artifacts and defend the design orally to a technical audience. The assignment emphasizes professional communication of methods, limitations, tradeoffs, and evidence.

CLO 6 Mastery Badge

Create beyond provided methods

Identify, evaluate, and create experimental methods or instrumentation beyond those provided by the instructor to foster independent professional growth.

What each sub-CLO requires

  • CLO6.1 — Identify Methods Beyond Those Provided

    Proficient: Find a relevant outside method, instrument, standard, platform, or analysis approach and explain its relevance using credible sources.
    Mastery: Search from a specific technical question, identify alternatives that address a real gap, and distinguish authoritative evidence from superficial claims.

  • CLO6.2 — Evaluate Methods Beyond Those Provided

    Proficient: Compare an outside method with the provided approach using relevant technical and practical criteria.
    Mastery: Evaluate limitations, assumptions, evidence quality, uncertainty consequences, integration effects, and failure modes, then explain when adoption would or would not help.

  • CLO6.3 — Create Methods Beyond Those Provided

    Proficient: Propose or synthesize a feasible method, instrumentation approach, analysis workflow, or extension beyond the provided course materials.
    Mastery: Independently create or adapt a method that integrates model, measurement, uncertainty, signal or DAQ, validation, and practical constraints.

Major summative assessment opportunities

  • Tier 2: Predict, Execute, Analyze, ImproveWeeks 8-11

    Students propose specific improvements to an existing experiment, tied to evidence from model comparison, instrumentation limits, or measurement strategy. This begins the shift from using provided methods to evaluating how methods could be better.

  • Final Lab: Paper Experimental Design and V&V PlanWeeks 12-15

    This is the primary CLO 6 demonstration: students must investigate at least one sensor, method, platform, standard, or analysis approach beyond the provided course catalog. They compare alternatives and justify whether the outside method improves the design.
