Overview

Overview - MTS EXCEED Tension Testing

In this lab, you will characterize the elastic-plastic deformation and mechanical response of structural metal alloy specimens using the MTS EXCEED electromechanical testing system. The provided source PDF identifies uniaxial tensile testing as the method used to estimate material parameters including Young's modulus, yield strength, ultimate tensile strength, and ductility.

A major focus of the lab is comparing two different displacement or strain measurements:

  • raw crosshead displacement from the testing machine, which can include frame compliance, load-cell elasticity, grip motion, and specimen slip
  • localized strain from an Epsilon 3542 axial extensometer mounted on the specimen gage section

Your job is to use the MTS TestSuite Elite (TW Elite) exported data to evaluate how the measurement method changes apparent material properties and to reason about machine compliance.

By the end of the lab, you should be able to:

  • safely operate the MTS EXCEED load frame, manual jog paddle, emergency stop, and protective interlock systems under teaching-team supervision
  • connect and verify the Epsilon 3542 axial extensometer using its TEDS calibration profile
  • conduct or analyze a uniaxial tensile test to failure while monitoring load, crosshead displacement, time, and extensometer strain
  • convert load and specimen dimensions into engineering stress
  • compare crosshead-based nominal strain against extensometer-based local strain
  • use the provided, unverified compliance model to estimate or discuss system compliance and apparent modulus

Procedure

Procedure - MTS EXCEED Tension Testing

Overview

This lab investigates uniaxial tensile testing using the MTS EXCEED electromechanical load frame and MTS TestSuite Elite (TW Elite). Students use an Epsilon 3542 axial extensometer when assigned, export raw CSV telemetry, and compare machine crosshead displacement with local extensometer strain.

The lab has two distinct parts that must be done in sequence:

  1. Pre-Lab: Review the extensometer limits, verify the provided unverified compliance derivation, and prepare an analysis script for exported CSV data.
  2. In-Lab: Set up the machine, verify the extensometer TEDS profile, grip the specimen, run the tension test, export data, and remove the specimen safely.

Part 1 - Pre-Lab

Before arriving at the MTS station:

  • Review the physical and electrical limits of the Epsilon 3542 axial extensometer.
  • Confirm the nominal extensometer gage length: 1.000 in.
  • Confirm the measuring range stated in the source PDF: +25% / -10% strain.
  • Review the unverified compliance derivation in @@FORWARD_MD_0@@.
  • Check all units in the compliance model, especially the units of machine compliance Cm.
  • Prepare a MATLAB or Python script that can load a CSV file containing Index, Time, Crosshead, Load, and Strain.
  • Prepare calculations for stress, nominal crosshead strain, and extensometer strain.

Part 3 - Critical Safety Requirements

Follow teaching-team instructions at the machine. The source PDF states these requirements:

  1. Wear approved safety glasses and closed-toe shoes.
  2. Keep hair tied securely off your face.
  3. Maintain awareness of vertical pinch zones immediately above and below the specimen and grip area.
  4. Locate the frame emergency stop and software emergency stops before powering or jogging the machine.
  5. Use a dual-operator setup during specimen loading and extensometer adjustment: one student works at the specimen while a partner stands ready at the emergency stop.
  6. Use manual remote paddle jog control during setup.
  7. Keep the front protective acrylic shield closed during active loading.

Part 4 - System Power and Software Connection

  1. Sign in to the laboratory computer using the student login.
  2. Open MTS TestSuite Elite, labeled TW Elite, from the desktop.
  3. Turn on the machine using the lower-right power knob, located left of the emergency stop.
  4. Rotate the knob clockwise until it stops and clicks.
  5. In TW Elite, open the Controller menu and select Connect.
  6. In the controller status bar, press the rotating counterclockwise refresh arrow.
  7. Confirm that the controller status changes from the red warning state to the green connected state before proceeding.

Part 5 - Extensometer Setup and TEDS Verification

  1. Plug the extensometer into Port J9 on the machine using the Bendix-to-Ethernet adapter.
  2. If Port J9 is difficult to locate, ask the teaching staff before continuing.
  3. In TW Elite, open Controller and select TEDS Devices....
  4. Verify that _Strain1 has a TED source path matching the Epsilon 3542 profile, such as C:\MTSTestSuite\Devices\_Strain1(epsilon3542_E103626).ted or an approved variation.
  5. Highlight _Strain1 and select OK.
  6. If the TEDS path does not match the assigned extensometer profile, stop and ask teaching staff for help.

  4. If the assigned run uses the extensometer, select MTS EM Tension (extensometer)_ASEN3501.

Part 7 - Specimen and Grip Setup

  1. Confirm which grips and inserts are installed: manual or pneumatic, flat or round.
  2. For the ASEN 3501 basic axial loading lab, the source PDF states that the grips are already attached to the machine.
  3. On the paddle to the right of the EXCEED machine, hold the unlock/lock button until the green light remains on. This enters manual mode.
  4. Loosen the bottom grip, insert the specimen, and tighten the bottom grip.
  5. Manually lower the top grip slightly lower than the final needed position because the grips move up when tightened.
  6. Do not tighten the top grip yet.

Part 8 - Install and Zero the Extensometer, If Used

  1. Attach the extensometer to the specimen using the built-in spring clamps and the proper grips for the specimen geometry.
  2. Leave enough space so the extensometer will not be crushed by the grips during testing or setup.
  3. Confirm that the extensometer does not wiggle excessively on the specimen.
  4. Once it is properly installed, remove the zeroing pin.
  5. In TW Elite, right-click the Extensometer monitor and select Zero Signal.
  6. If the extensometer is jostled later, reinstall the zeroing pin and repeat the installation procedure.

Part 9 - Complete Specimen Setup

  1. In TW Elite, right-click the lower-left Load monitor and select Zero Signal.
  2. Tighten the top grip until the Load monitor reads 178-222 N (40-50 lbf).
  3. Do not zero the load after applying this clamping force.
  4. Zero the crosshead measurement by right-clicking the middle monitor and selecting Zero Signal.
  5. Manually jog the crosshead up and down to seat the grips.
  6. Use the paddle scroll wheel for fine movement.
  7. Keep the applied force change within 445-660 N (100-150 lbf).
  8. Return the crosshead to its zero value.
  9. Do not zero the software after returning the crosshead.
  10. Return control from the paddle to the software by holding the lock button until the light turns off.
  11. Close the front protective shield.

Part 11 - Running the Experiment

  1. Press the green run arrow in the Test control area.
  2. When the setup window appears, enter the specimen width, thickness, and rate.
  3. Select OK.
  4. Monitor the test while a partner remains ready to hit the emergency stop if needed.
  5. When the test finishes and the software asks Return to Zero?, select NO.
  6. Review the recorded data before exporting.

Part 12 - Data Export

  1. Open the File menu.
  2. Select Export, then Raw Data....
  3. Choose One File per test-run.
  4. Set the folder path to Downloads or Desktop for the logged-in student account.
  5. Leave Create new folder on for Folder Save.
  6. Select Comma separated values for the format.
  7. Select the assigned unit set; the PDF recommends SI (mm-N) or SI (mm-kN).
  8. Confirm that the signal list includes Crosshead, Load, Time, and Extensometer if used.
  9. Select Export.
  10. Transfer the exported file to secure storage before signing out.
  11. Confirm the transferred file opens and contains the expected data.

Do not leave your only copy on the laboratory computer. Local files may be erased.

Part 13 - Specimen Removal, Shutdown, and Cleanup

If the specimen was tested to failure:

  1. Remove the extensometer, if used, and return it to the proper storage container.
  2. Unlock the paddle as needed.
  3. Unscrew each grip and remove the specimen pieces.

If the specimen was not tested to failure:

  1. Remove the extensometer, if used, and return it to the proper storage container.
  2. Unlock the paddle as needed.
  3. Return the crosshead to zero to relieve specimen stress.
  4. Loosen the top grip, then loosen the bottom grip and remove the specimen.

For shutdown:

  1. Give all broken or unbroken specimens to teaching staff for proper disposal.
  2. Confirm data are saved and transferred.
  3. Confirm all specimens have been removed from the machine.
  4. Close TW Elite and sign out of the computer.
  5. Turn off the machine using the lower-right power knob.

Unverified Derivation

Unverified Derivation - MTS EXCEED Compliance Model

How Students Should Use This Derivation

This derivation is provided so the laboratory exercise can focus on experimental data analysis, model implementation, and model-data comparison. It is not externally verified by the laboratory staff in the current source PDF.

Students are required to verify the derivation and assumptions before using it in final analysis. Verification means checking dimensions, elastic variables, boundary conditions, and whether a simple series-compliance model is adequate for the physical MTS EXCEED testing apparatus.

Stress Definition

An axial load P is applied to a specimen with initial width w and thickness t.


A0 = w t
sigma = P / A0

where:

SymbolMeaningUnits
A0Initial cross-sectional areaarea units consistent with w and t
PApplied axial loadforce
sigmaEngineering stressforce / area

Two Strain Measures

The source PDF distinguishes two measures of strain.

Nominal Crosshead Strain

Nominal strain is derived from total crosshead displacement relative to the initial free grip-to-grip specimen length:


epsilon_n = delta_total / Lg

Extensometer Strain

Extensometer strain is measured locally by the Epsilon extensometer over its fixed gage length:


epsilon_e = DeltaLe / L0

For the Epsilon 3542 axial extensometer in this PDF:


L0 = 1.000 in

Total Crosshead Displacement Model

The crosshead displacement includes more than specimen stretch. The source PDF identifies machine frame compliance, load-cell elasticity, grip/specimen interface motion, and possible specimen slip as contributors.


delta_total = delta_specimen + delta_compliance + delta_slip

Assuming the system is in the linear-elastic regime before material yield and no slip occurs:


delta_slip = 0
delta_compliance = P Cm
delta_specimen = P Lg / (A0 E)

where:

SymbolMeaningUnits
CmMachine compliance parameterdisplacement / force, such as in/lb or mm/N
EMaterial elastic modulus from a better local strain measurementstress units
LgFree grip-to-grip specimen lengthlength

Substituting into the total displacement model gives:


delta_total = P Lg / (A0 E) + P Cm

Dividing by load:


delta_total / P = Lg / (A0 E) + Cm

Solving for machine compliance:


Cm = delta_total / P - Lg / (A0 E)
Cm = epsilon_n Lg / P - Lg / (A0 E)

Apparent Elastic Modulus Relation

If students compute elastic modulus using nominal strain from crosshead displacement, they obtain an apparent modulus Eapp. The source PDF gives the series-spring relation:


1 / Eapp = 1 / E + Cm (A0 / Lg)

The PDF states that students should plot 1/Eapp against A0/Lg across multiple specimens or free lengths, then check whether the slope gives a consistent Cm and the intercept matches 1/E from the extensometer.

Placeholder requiring teaching-team decision: the current PDF only gives one nominal specimen width and thickness for the basic ASEN 3501 setup. If students are expected to estimate Cm from a slope, the assigned data must include multiple specimens, multiple free lengths, or another explicit method for compliance estimation.

Verification Checks

Before relying on this model, verify:

  • sigma = P/A0 uses consistent force and area units
  • epsilon_n and epsilon_e are dimensionless
  • Cm has units of displacement per force
  • the no-slip assumption is reasonable for the selected elastic-region data
  • the elastic-region data are selected before yield
  • crosshead displacement and extensometer strain are not treated as interchangeable measurements
  • preload and grip seating steps do not hide or erase real specimen load history

FAQs

FAQs - MTS EXCEED Tension Testing

Do I need to derive the compliance model from scratch?

No. The derivation is provided, but it is explicitly unverified. Your task is to check the assumptions, units, and physical meaning before using it.

Why do crosshead displacement and extensometer strain differ?

The crosshead measurement includes more than local specimen stretch. It can include machine frame compliance, load-cell elasticity, grip motion, and specimen slip. The extensometer is intended to measure local strain over its gage length.

What extensometer should I verify in TW Elite?

The source PDF identifies the Epsilon 3542 axial extensometer, Part No. 3542-0100-025-ST, with a 1.000 in gage length. In the TEDS Devices window, verify that _Strain1 points to the assigned Epsilon 3542 profile.

What should I do if the TEDS path does not match?

Stop and ask teaching staff. Do not proceed with a mismatched or missing extensometer calibration profile.

Should I zero the load after tightening the top grip?

No. The source PDF explicitly warns not to zero the load after applying the 178-222 N (40-50 lbf) top-grip preload because that force is already applied to the specimen.

What data columns should I expect after export?

The PDF lists Index, Time, Crosshead, Load, and Strain. The Strain channel is expected when the extensometer is used.
