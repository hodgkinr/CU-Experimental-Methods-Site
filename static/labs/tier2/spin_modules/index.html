Overview

Overview - Spin Modules

Students use three distinct data sets in the Tier 2 spin investigation. Existing SC (bare-spacecraft) records show the free base driven by the base motor and are used to fit base support inertia and effective dynamic retardance. New RW_freespin records show the base responding while reaction-wheel torque is applied; students use them to fit effective wheel-side retardance and compare measured base acceleration with the model. Historical RW_* records hold the base stationary and are used only for RW torque/current and wheel-speed characterization.

The wheel torque balance is

\[ |\tau_{m,RW}|=1000I_{RW}|\alpha_{RW}|+\tau_{r,RW}. \]

The current data reduction provisionally treats logged wheel rate as relative to the base. Assuming positive wheel spin and opposite base acceleration, \(\alpha_{RW}=\alpha_{RW,relative}-|\alpha_{base}|\). Verify the channel reference/sign before treating this correction as established. The fitted wheel-side retardance is an effective torque-balance intercept, not a calibrated bearing-friction value.

For base acceleration on the moving branch, use

\[ |\alpha_{base}|=\frac{|\tau_{m,RW}|-\tau_{r,RW}-\tau_{r,base}}{1000I_{fit}}, \qquad |\tau_{m,RW}|>\tau_{r,RW}+\tau_{r,base}. \]

Torque/retardance is in mN m, inertia in kg m\(^2\), acceleration in rad/s\(^2\), and 1000 converts mN m to N m. The motor reaction on the base opposes wheel motor torque. Wheel-side drag reacts on the base in the direction that reduces this motor reaction; base retardance further opposes base motion. At or below combined retardance, this moving equation does not predict static breakaway.

Current reference analysis gives \(\tau_{r,base}\approx2.319\) mN m, \(\tau_{r,RW}\approx2.838\) mN m, and combined nominal retardance about 5.16 mN m. The corrected branch is sufficiently probable for residual comparison at only four of the seven free-base points. Those four residuals remain negative (mean about −0.175 rad/s\(^2\)); their pooled residual discrepancy RMS is about 0.179 rad/s\(^2\). The wheel-loss fit and base-response comparison use the same runs, with one run per torque, so this is an initial model check rather than an independent holdout validation or repeatability estimate.

Using SC support-fit inertia and base retardance in the internal RW case remains provisional. The dynamics derivation separates base/spacecraft and wheel inertia, and available layout information does not establish the equivalence. Students should distinguish conditional model uncertainty from the residual-based diagnostic and keep unresolved transfer, current, encoder, and repeatability uncertainties visible. A ≤25% uncertainty criterion is a selected decision rule, not proof of validation or universal safety.

The working 3000 rpm wheel limit depends on torque, duration, and initial speed; it is not a torque ceiling. Gyro comparison and wheel-speed feasibility are optional extensions. A gyro may change empirical acceleration uncertainty but does not automatically change theoretical model/input uncertainty.

Unverified Derivation

Derivation: SC support fit and free-base reaction-wheel response

1. Identify the SC support response

Choose positive rotation along the measured SC base-motion direction. Dynamic resistance acts opposite motion:

\[ \tau_{base}=I_{fit}\alpha_{base}+\tau_{r,base}\operatorname{sgn}(\omega_{base}). \]

For the positive-direction support trials, express torque in mN\,m as

\[ \tau_{base,\mathrm{mN\,m}}=1000I_{fit}\alpha_{base}+\tau_{r,base}. \]

Estimate \(\alpha_{base}\) from the slope of encoder rate converted from rpm to rad/s against actual timestamp in seconds. Estimate support torque from baseline-corrected base-motor current and the base-motor constant. Fit the five SC records together and inspect the residuals. \(\tau_{r,base}\) is an effective dynamic retarding torque for the support configuration. Static breakaway is not measured.

2. Fit wheel-side losses from free-base RW records

For each RW_freespin run, infer RW motor torque from pre-command-baseline-corrected RW current and the RW motor torque constant. The wheel torque balance during spin-up is

\[ |\tau_{m,RW}|=1000I_{RW}|\alpha_{RW}|+\tau_{r,RW}. \]

This regression estimates effective rotating inertia \(I_{RW}\) and effective wheel-side retardance \(\tau_{r,RW}\). The logged RW rate is provisionally interpreted as wheel rate relative to the base. With the base accelerating opposite positive wheel spin, \(\alpha_{RW}=\alpha_{RW,relative}-|\alpha_{base}|\). The sign and reference of this channel need confirmation. Fit sensitivity without the base correction is useful as a check, but cannot establish the encoder convention. Since torque is current-inferred and no independent calibration is supplied, the intercept is not pure bearing friction: it may absorb other wheel-side losses or torque/current bias. Treat constant retardance as a model assumption.

Current analysis reference: \(I_{RW}\approx1.2785\times10^{-4}\) kg\,m\(^2\), \(\tau_{r,RW}\approx2.838\) mN\,m, with conditional Monte Carlo 95% intervals approximately \([1.2423,1.3154]\times10^{-4}\) kg\,m\(^2\) and 2.699–2.977 mN\,m. Omitting the base-acceleration correction changes the fit to about \(1.2602\times10^{-4}\) kg\,m\(^2\) and 2.860 mN\,m. These intervals do not include unquantified current-channel calibration or encoder systematic uncertainty.

3. Derive net base torque and moving-branch prediction

Take positive direction along wheel spin. The motor applies positive \(\tau_{m,RW}\) to the rotor and an equal-and-opposite torque to the base. Wheel drag opposes wheel spin, so the drag reaction on the stator/base acts along wheel spin, reducing the opposite motor-reaction torque available to accelerate the base. For a base moving opposite wheel spin, base retardance opposes that base motion. The net torque magnitude in the modeled direction is therefore

\[ |\tau_{base,net}|=|\tau_{m,RW}|-\tau_{r,RW}-\tau_{r,base}. \]

Using the SC support-fit inertia provisionally gives

\[ |\alpha_{base}|=\frac{|\tau_{m,RW}|-\tau_{r,RW}-\tau_{r,base}}{1000I_{fit}}, \quad |\tau_{m,RW}|>\tau_{r,RW}+\tau_{r,base}. \]

All torque terms are in mN\,m, \(I_{fit}\) is in kg\,m\(^2\), and acceleration is in rad/s\(^2\). The factor 1000 converts mN\,m to N\,m. With current fitted retardances, the combined nominal threshold is about 5.16 mN\,m. At or below it, this moving-branch equation does not predict motion onset, static breakaway, or reversal.

4. What the measured comparison shows

The seven free-base records have one run each at nominal command labels 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, and 12 mN\,m. Current-inferred torque is approximately 3.408–11.761 mN\,m. The corrected model's moving branch meets the 99% probability reporting condition only for the 6, 8, 10, and 12 mN\,m labels. On those four points, measured-minus-predicted residual mean is about −0.175 rad/s\(^2\), raw RMS about 0.202 rad/s\(^2\), and pooled residual discrepancy RMS after subtracting measured/model variances in quadrature about 0.179 rad/s\(^2\). All four residuals remain negative. On these same four points, the model before adding RW retardance had mean residual about −0.683 rad/s\(^2\), so the added loss materially improves the fit but does not remove its systematic discrepancy.

The pooled residual scale includes bias and is based on only four one-run observations. It is not torque-specific repeatability, a validated stochastic model, or proof that the transfer is correct. The first three points are excluded because the corrected model does not have a sufficiently probable moving branch; do not assign them corrected-model residual uncertainty.

5. Transfer assumption and angular momentum

The supplied dynamics reference distinguishes base/spacecraft inertia \(I_s\) and wheel inertia \(I_w\), with \(I_s\dot\omega_s+I_w\dot\omega_w=0\) when external torque is negligible. The SC fit estimates an effective inertia in a base-motor support configuration. The hardware layout does not establish that this equals the base inertia appropriate to internal RW torque. The fitted base retardance may also change with configuration, bearing load, or speed. Thus the base-parameter transfer remains provisional despite the improved measured match. Resolve it through hardware/mounting evidence and repeated, calibrated free-base tests across torque directions and operating range.

6. Torque constants and uncertainty roles

The base motor constant converts SC base current to support torque; the RW motor constant converts RW motor current to input torque and to wheel-side torque balance. Keep the constants separate. Manufacturer datasheets list nominal 25.5 mN\,m/A for base motor part 200142 and 33.5 mN\,m/A for RW motor part 251601. Neither specifies the working ±0.05 mN\,m/A half-width used in the current analysis. Do not present it as a verified calibration uncertainty.

The current first-pass calculation propagates SC and RW rate-fit uncertainty, the assumed torque-constant bounds, and fit residual scatter. Current-channel calibration, timing, encoder scale/sign/systematics, between-run repeatability, correlations, and the SC-to-RW transfer discrepancy remain unquantified or excluded. Current/timing terms are treated as negligible only by explicit assumption. Label the conditional uncertainty curve accordingly.

7. Additional validation data

Repeat free-base tests with independently calibrated or measured signed RW torque, a documented wheel-rate reference/sign, base encoder rate, synchronized timestamps, command/status channels, and repeated trials in both torque directions. Sample below, near, and above the combined retardance and across the intended operating region. Compare the base acceleration, wheel acceleration, and wheel/base momentum exchange with the model; separate parameter-transfer bias from wheel loss, base loss, and run-to-run scatter.

Working derivation. Confirm rotating assembly and encoder channel conventions with the hardware owner before treating parameter transfer as established.

Spin Modules Tier 2: SC support fit, RW loss fit, and base-response prediction

Data roles and analysis objective

Use three data sets for distinct purposes:

  1. The five SC support records (base motor drives the free base) identify the support-fit inertia and effective base retardance.
  2. Seven RW_freespin records (reaction-wheel motor spins the wheel while the base is free) provide wheel current/rate and measured base-rate response. They support an effective wheel-side torque-loss fit and an empirical acceleration/model comparison.
  3. Historical RW_* records hold the base stationary. Use them for RW current/torque and wheel-speed characterization only; their base-rate channel is not a free-base response.

The current free-base records are one run at each of seven torque labels, without independent current/torque calibration metadata or documented repeats. The wheel-loss fit and base-response comparison use these same runs, so the residual comparison is not an independent holdout validation. Treat it as an initial model check, not as a validated uncertainty distribution or repeatability study.

SC support fit

For a base moving in a signed direction, dynamic retardance opposes base motion:

\[ \tau_{base}=1000 I_{fit}\alpha_{base}+\tau_{r,base}\,\operatorname{sgn}(\omega_{base}). \]

For the positive-direction SC records this reduces to

\[ \tau_{base,\mathrm{mN\,m}}=1000 I_{fit}\alpha_{base}+\tau_{r,base}. \]

Torque and retardance are in mN\,m, inertia in kg\,m\(^2\), acceleration in rad/s\(^2\); 1000 converts mN\,m to N\,m. Estimate SC support torque from baseline-corrected base-motor current using \(K_{t,base}\). Estimate acceleration from the slope of encoder rate in rad/s versus actual timestamp in seconds. The fitted retardance is an effective moving-branch loss, not a separately measured static breakaway torque.

RW spin-up torque balance

During RW spin-up, estimate wheel motor torque from baseline-corrected RW-motor current using its separate constant \(K_{t,RW}\). Fit

\[ |\tau_{m,RW}|=1000 I_{RW}|\alpha_{RW}|+\tau_{r,RW}. \]

Here \(I_{RW}\) is an effective wheel rotating inertia and \(\tau_{r,RW}\) is an effective wheel-side retarding torque. The logged rate in the free-base records is treated provisionally as wheel rate relative to the base. If the wheel accelerates positive while the base accelerates oppositely, then

\[ \alpha_{RW}=\alpha_{RW,relative}-|\alpha_{base}|. \]

Confirm the encoder reference and sign mapping from hardware/channel documentation. The intercept is not an independently calibrated bearing-friction value: it can include multiple wheel-side losses and torque-constant/current bias. The fit assumes approximately constant retardance over the measured spin-up conditions.

Base response to internal RW motor torque

Define positive direction along wheel spin. The motor applies \(+\tau_{m,RW}\) to the wheel and an equal-and-opposite reaction \(-\tau_{m,RW}\) to the base. Wheel-side drag opposes wheel spin; its equal-and-opposite reaction on the base points along wheel spin and reduces the magnitude of the motor reaction that accelerates the base in the opposite direction. Base retardance further opposes base motion. When the motor reaction dominates both losses, the moving-branch magnitude equation is

\[ |\alpha_{base}|=\frac{|\tau_{m,RW}|-\tau_{r,RW}-\tau_{r,base}}{1000 I_{fit}}, \qquad |\tau_{m,RW}|>\tau_{r,RW}+\tau_{r,base}. \]

The base accelerates opposite the RW motor torque. The expression is undefined at or below combined retardance and does not predict whether a stationary base begins moving, static breakaway, or torque reversal. The equation also assumes that a single constant effective retardance for each side is adequate over the analyzed branch.

Unresolved parameter transfer

The analysis provisionally substitutes the SC support-fit \(I_{fit}\) and \(\tau_{r,base}\) into the internal RW case. The supplied dynamics derivation distinguishes base/spacecraft inertia \(I_s\) from wheel inertia \(I_w\), with internal angular-momentum exchange \(I_s\dot\omega_s+I_w\dot\omega_w=0\) when external torque is negligible. Available layout information does not establish that the base-motor support test and the internal RW test involve the same rotating assembly/effective inertia. The free-base measurements provide a comparison, but do not prove this transfer or resolve unmodeled coupling. Keep the assumption visible and state what additional hardware evidence or calibrated repeated testing would resolve it.

First-pass uncertainty and decision rule

The working analysis propagates SC slope errors, assumed base/RW motor-constant bounds, SC support-fit residual scatter, free-base rate-fit errors, and RW torque-balance fit residual scatter. The assumed \(\pm0.05\) mN\,m/A torque-constant half-width is not supplied as a manufacturer tolerance or calibration uncertainty. Current-channel calibration, encoder scale/sign systematic uncertainty, timing, between-run repeatability, and SC-to-internal-RW transfer discrepancy are not quantified. Current and timing contributions may be treated as negligible only if labeled as first-pass assumptions.

Plot conditional predicted relative standard uncertainty, \(100u_\alpha/|\alpha_{base}|\), versus applied RW torque. For branch-valid residuals \(e_i=\alpha_{meas,i}-\alpha_{model,i}\), the current empirical diagnostic uses

\[ u_{disc}=\sqrt{\max\left(0,\operatorname{mean}\left[e_i^2-u_{meas,i}^2-u_{model,i}^2\right]\right)},\qquad u_{emp,i}=\sqrt{u_{model,i}^2+u_{disc}^2}. \]

Plot \(100u_{emp,i}/|\alpha_{model,i}|\) separately from the conditional theoretical curve. This pooled scale uses only torque points with a sufficiently probable corrected moving branch. It includes model bias and one run per usable torque, so it is diagnostic, not torque-specific repeatability or a validated probability model. Apply the selected \(\leq25\%\) criterion to the conditional model curve as an analysis rule, not as validation, universal safety, or an operating approval. Clearly mark the moving branch, low-probability/undefined conditions, and extrapolation beyond the SC support-torque range.

Current reference result (for comparison, not a guaranteed outcome)

The current MATLAB reduction estimates \(\tau_{r,base}\approx2.319\) mN\,m, \(\tau_{r,RW}\approx2.838\) mN\,m, and \(I_{RW}\approx1.278\times10^{-4}\) kg\,m\(^2\). The RW-retardance Monte Carlo interval is approximately 2.70–2.98 mN\,m under the working model and assumed uncertainties. The combined nominal retardance is about 5.16 mN\,m. Only the four records labeled 6, 8, 10, and 12 mN\,m meet the 99% moving-branch probability reporting condition. Their mean residual is about −0.175 rad/s\(^2\), raw RMS 0.202 rad/s\(^2\), and pooled residual discrepancy RMS 0.179 rad/s\(^2\); the measured accelerations remain below the model. These values inherit the unresolved encoder, current, repeatability, and transfer assumptions above.

Optional extensions

  • Wheel speed: The working 3000 rpm limit constrains torque together with duration and initial speed. It is not a universal torque ceiling. Historical fixture-held speeds do not establish the speed trajectory during a free-base run.
  • MEMS gyro: A gyro may change empirical acceleration uncertainty through noise, bias, scale, filtering, timing, and calibration. It does not automatically change the theoretical uncertainty from model parameters and torque inputs.

Procedure

Procedure - Spin Modules Tier 2: SC support and RW free-base response

Objective

Fit the SC base-motor support response, estimate effective wheel-side retardance from the supplied free-base reaction-wheel records, then compare the measured base acceleration with a two-loss prediction. Produce conditional model uncertainty and a separate residual-based diagnostic versus applied RW torque. The existing RW_freespin data provide an initial comparison, but one run per torque is not enough to establish repeatability or validate the parameter transfer. Historical RW_* data hold the base stationary and are for RW torque/current and wheel-speed characterization only.

Before analysis or operation

Follow instructor power-up, wind-down, clamping, and motor-command instructions. Confirm setup, channel labels, sign conventions, units, and timestamp meaning. Preserve original files and document all processing. Use only instructor-approved limits. Working limits of ±2 A motor current and 3000 rpm wheel speed require confirmation; 3000 rpm is a speed limit, not a torque ceiling. Do not infer a limit or safety margin from these data.

A. SC base-motor support fit

  1. Load the SC records at nominal base-motor torque labels 4, 5, 6, 8, and 10 mN\,m. Keep configuration labels and source files unchanged. Before inspecting the free-base RW response records, freeze the SC-only baseline prediction and save it for comparison with the later wheel-loss model.
  2. Identify time, base-motor command/current, and base encoder rate. Confirm units/sign and inspect timestamps, gaps, dropouts, clipping, and command transitions.
  3. Estimate base-current zero using only pre-command samples. Calculate support torque using the base-motor nominal constant, 25.5 mN\,m/A. If using ±0.05 mN\,m/A, label it a working bound; the datasheet does not state this uncertainty. Current-channel accuracy is unresolved and is negligible only by first-pass assumption.
  4. Convert encoder rate from rpm to rad/s. Fit rate against actual timestamps over the documented steady interval (the current analysis uses 1.5–5.5 s). Report the fit window, sample count, time gaps, fit residuals, and within-record slope-uncertainty method.
  5. Fit \(\tau_{base,\mathrm{mN\,m}}=1000I_{fit}\alpha_{base}+\tau_{r,base}\). Report units and residuals. Treat \(\tau_{r,base}\) as effective dynamic retardance, not static breakaway.

B. Free-base RW torque and wheel-loss fit

  1. Load RW_freespin records at nominal labels 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, and 12 mN\,m. Check that the RW command is active in the analysis window and base command is zero. Preserve each record as a single run; do not present these seven records as replicated trials.
  2. Determine the pre-command RW current baseline and calculate current-inferred RW motor torque using the RW-motor nominal constant, 33.5 mN\,m/A. Keep command torque and current-inferred torque distinct. The ±0.05 mN\,m/A working bound is an assumption, not a datasheet calibration tolerance.
  3. Fit base-rate and wheel-rate slopes against actual timestamps in the steady interval. Confirm the RW encoder reference/sign from available hardware/channel information. The current analysis provisionally treats logged RW rate as wheel rate relative to the base and, when base motion is opposite wheel spin, calculates \(\alpha_{RW}=\alpha_{RW,relative}-|\alpha_{base}|\). Document this assumption and include the fit sensitivity without the correction if the reference cannot be confirmed.
  4. Fit \(|\tau_{m,RW}|=1000I_{RW}|\alpha_{RW}|+\tau_{r,RW}\). Call the intercept an effective wheel-side retarding torque: the records do not isolate bearing friction from other losses or current/torque bias. State the constant-retardance assumption and the single-run limitation.

C. Corrected base-response model and comparison

  1. Use the sign convention that positive motor torque accelerates the wheel and produces equal-and-opposite motor reaction on the base. Wheel-side drag opposes wheel spin; its reaction on the base reduces the magnitude of the motor reaction that accelerates the base. Base retardance further opposes base motion. For the moving branch, use

\[ |\alpha_{base}|=\frac{|\tau_{m,RW}|-\tau_{r,RW}-\tau_{r,base}}{1000I_{fit}},\qquad |\tau_{m,RW}|>\tau_{r,RW}+\tau_{r,base}. \]

Torque and retardance are mN\,m, inertia is kg\,m\(^2\), and acceleration is rad/s\(^2\); 1000 converts mN\,m to N\,m. Base acceleration is opposite wheel motor torque. At/below the combined retardance, the equation does not describe breakaway or stationary behavior.

  1. Carry SC support-fit \(I_{fit}\) and \(\tau_{r,base}\) into the internal-RW case only as a transfer assumption. The dynamics derivation treats wheel inertia separately in internal angular-momentum exchange, and hardware documentation does not establish that support-fit inertia is the correct base-response inertia.
  2. Plot measured base acceleration magnitude versus current-inferred RW motor torque. Overlay the corrected model and its conditional uncertainty band. Show the earlier no-RW-retardance curve as a comparison if helpful. Show vertical bars as within-record OLS slope SEs and horizontal bars as only the assumed RW torque-constant bound; do not imply they include calibration or repeatability.
  3. Calculate measured-minus-predicted residuals only where the corrected model moving branch has the predeclared probability required by the analysis (the current reference uses at least 99%). Report the residual mean and raw RMS. A pooled RMS after subtracting within-record/model variances in quadrature may be plotted as a residual-inflated empirical error scale. State that it includes systematic bias, pools only branch-valid points, and is not torque-specific repeatability or a validated distribution. Because the wheel-loss fit and base-response comparison use the same seven runs, this is not an independent holdout validation.

D. Uncertainty plot and decision

  1. Propagate the specified first-pass terms: SC acceleration-slope errors; the assumed base-motor torque-constant bound in support fitting; SC support residual scatter; RW acceleration/current-inferred torque uncertainty and RW torque-balance fit scatter; and the assumed RW torque-constant bound. State distributions, correlations/independence assumptions, and method. Current and DAQ/timing uncertainty may be treated as negligible only by explicit first-pass assumption. Do not invent encoder or current accuracy values.
  2. Produce the conditional model plot of relative standard uncertainty in predicted base acceleration, percent, versus applied RW torque magnitude. Plot the residual-inflated empirical error scale separately at branch-valid measured torque points. Mark moving-branch validity, the combined-retardance threshold, and interpolation/extrapolation relative to SC support torque.
  3. Apply the working decision rule “good” = conditional relative standard uncertainty ≤25%, unless the instructor supplies a replacement rule. State that this is a chosen criterion, not validation, safety, or proof of agreement with measurements. The present measured residual bias and unresolved inertia transfer prevent using this criterion alone as an operating recommendation.

Further validation data

The supplied free-base runs give an initial comparison. Stronger validation needs repeated runs in both torque directions below, near, and above the combined retardance and through the intended operating region. Record independently calibrated/signed RW torque or current, wheel speed with a documented absolute/relative reference, base encoder rate, synchronized timestamps, command transitions, status/dropout flags, and trial identifiers. Predeclare the fit window. Quantify current/torque, timing, encoder scale/rate processing, and between-run uncertainty so residual bias, friction variation, parameter-transfer discrepancy, and repeatability can be separated.

Optional advanced extensions

  • 3000 rpm wheel-speed comparison: Feasibility depends on torque, duration, and initial speed. Use instructor-confirmed limit and margin; do not treat 3000 rpm as a torque ceiling.
  • MEMS gyro comparison: A gyro may change measured acceleration uncertainty through its noise, bias, scale, filter, synchronization, and calibration. It does not automatically change the theoretical uncertainty due to model parameters and torque inputs.

Required products

  • SC support-fit table and residual summary.
  • RW current-inferred motor torque, wheel-rate reduction, and effective wheel-retardance fit with sign/reference assumption and sensitivity check.
  • Measured free-base acceleration/model comparison with declared error bars and branch-valid residual table.
  • Conditional relative-uncertainty plot and a separate residual-based diagnostic curve.
  • Candidate-region decision using the selected rule, with measurement/model discrepancies and uncertainty-source status.
  • Data specification for stronger repeated free-base RW validation.

FAQs

FAQs - Spin Modules Tier 2

What is the core task?

Fit the SC base-motor support response, estimate effective wheel-side retardance from the free-base RW_freespin records, compare measured base acceleration with the corrected model, and produce conditional and residual-based uncertainty plots versus RW torque. Keep fit assumptions and unresolved uncertainty sources explicit.

Which RW records measure base acceleration?

The separate RW_freespin records provide one free-base response record at each of seven torque labels, based on the supplied setup description. The historical RW_* records hold the base stationary: use them for RW torque/current and wheel-speed characterization only. The free-base runs are a preliminary comparison, not replicated validation data.

What equations should I use?

SC support fit:

\[ \tau_{base,\mathrm{mN\,m}}=1000I_{fit}\alpha_{base}+\tau_{r,base}. \]

RW spin-up balance:

\[ |\tau_{m,RW}|=1000I_{RW}|\alpha_{RW}|+\tau_{r,RW}. \]

Corrected base moving branch:

\[ |\alpha_{base}|=\frac{|\tau_{m,RW}|-\tau_{r,RW}-\tau_{r,base}}{1000I_{fit}}, \qquad |\tau_{m,RW}|>\tau_{r,RW}+\tau_{r,base}. \]

Torque/retardance is mN m, inertia is kg m\(^2\), and acceleration is rad/s\(^2\); 1000 converts mN m to N m. The base acceleration is opposite wheel motor torque. Wheel-side drag reduces the net motor reaction available to accelerate the base. At/below combined retardance, the moving-branch equation does not determine breakaway or stationary behavior.

How is wheel acceleration calculated from the free-base records?

The current analysis provisionally treats logged wheel rate as wheel rate relative to base. If wheel spin is positive and base acceleration is opposite, it uses \(\alpha_{RW}=\alpha_{RW,relative}-|\alpha_{base}|\). Check the encoder reference and sign from hardware/channel documentation. A sensitivity fit without the correction is informative but does not verify the sign convention.

Is the fitted wheel retardance pure bearing friction?

No. It is an effective torque-balance intercept based on current-inferred motor torque and measured wheel acceleration. It may include bearing and other wheel-side losses as well as current or torque-constant bias. The constant-retardance fit is a simplified model assumption, not a separate bearing calibration.

What do the current data show?

The current analysis estimates base retardance near 2.319 mN m and wheel-side retardance near 2.838 mN m, for a combined nominal threshold near 5.16 mN m. The corrected model is sufficiently on its moving branch for four points (labels 6, 8, 10, 12 mN m). Their measured-minus-predicted residual mean is about −0.175 rad/s\(^2\), so the model still overpredicts. The pooled residual discrepancy RMS is about 0.179 rad/s\(^2\), based on four single runs; it includes bias and is not a repeatability estimate or validated stochastic uncertainty. The wheel-loss fit and comparison use the same runs, so this is not an independent holdout validation.

Why is using the SC-fitted inertia still an assumption?

The support fit comes from base-motor torque, while reaction-wheel motor torque is internal and exchanges angular momentum between base and wheel. The derivation treats wheel inertia separately, and the available layout does not prove that the SC support-fit inertia is the correct inertia for the base response to internal RW torque. The new comparison informs this assumption but does not establish it. Repeated free-base tests, documented hardware inertia, and calibrated torque are needed.

Which torque constant applies?

Use the base-motor constant only for SC support torque. Use the separate RW-motor constant for RW motor torque and its torque-balance fit. Datasheets list nominal constants 25.5 and 33.5 mN m/A, respectively, but do not specify the ±0.05 mN m/A working bounds. Those bounds are assumptions, not verified calibration uncertainties.

What uncertainty sources are included or unresolved?

The current first pass includes SC and RW rate-slope errors, assumed motor-constant bounds, and SC/RW fit residual scatter. Current and timing contributions are treated as negligible only by explicit first-pass assumption. Encoder scale, sign/reference, current calibration, between-run repeatability, and the SC-to-RW transfer discrepancy remain unresolved. Do not invent sensor accuracy values. The residual-based scale uses only four branch-valid single-run points and should not be presented as torque-specific repeatability.

What is a “good” torque region?

Use the working rule “good” = conditional model relative standard uncertainty ≤25%, unless the instructor supplies another threshold. It is a chosen uncertainty criterion, not validation, universal safety, or agreement with measured acceleration. Predictions outside the measured SC support-torque span are extrapolations. Consider the free-base residual bias and unresolved transfer before recommending a region.

What further data would strengthen validation?

Repeat free-base runs in both torque directions below, near, and above combined retardance and over the intended operating range. Record signed or calibrated RW torque/current, wheel speed with documented rate reference/sign, base encoder rate, synchronized timestamps, commands/status, and trial identifiers. Quantify current/torque, timing, encoder, and between-run uncertainties.

Are the 3000 rpm limit and MEMS gyro required?

No. Both are optional advanced extensions. Wheel-speed feasibility depends on torque, command duration, and initial speed; 3000 rpm is not a universal torque ceiling. A gyro may change empirical acceleration uncertainty but does not automatically change theoretical model/input uncertainty.

Is angular-momentum capacity or controller design required?

No. Angular momentum capacity, saturation-time analysis, panel comparison, and controller design are outside the bare-minimum scope unless assigned separately.
